## 2. What each player experienced

**Newcomer: Witch** (defaults: Broomstick, Familiar's Warning, Cook)
- Best: escaping a six-round chase over the rooftops, then opening the strongbox for the spoons next Turn. Worst: a 1 + 1 at the guard dog on her first roll of the night, then five Turns standing at the gate.
- **Downtime: 5 Turns** (Turns 5–9 before the flight). Her Cook Duty never applied, because the party skipped the butcher.
- Understood? The rolls and results, yes; At the Table was enough for those. She needed the table for *when* to spend a charge ("Do I spend one now?") and asked "What's the Limit again?" at the moment it mattered. She never used Broomstick.
- Felt like a Witch? Yes: the black cat, the rowan sprig in the chase, and Hedge Spell going on friends' rolls in the flight. She spent all three charges, two of them on other people.

**Daredevil: Ghost** (Fade, Spectral, Butler)
- Best: walking into the tailor's and through the crowd with Spectral, and leaving with the bandages in the same Turn. Worst: the final flight. Cold Iron (Always) put him a size down from round 1. He rolled a d4 on one dead end and needed the Witch's raise on the other, and he held a charge that could do nothing.
- **Downtime: 3 Turns** (5–7, waiting at the tailor to carry an armour that never came).
- Understood? Yes. He got his loud ways (the guard dog) and his furniture push, and he spent both charges on the trapdoor. He never let the Monster out unprotected; with Fade he didn't need to.
- Felt like a Ghost? Spectral did, strongly. Through the Wall never came up, because opening an approach would have stranded his partner.

**Planner: Werewolf** (Through the Hedge, Fetch, Handyman)
- Best: the lock. Through the Hedge turned the Wits-10 watchman into Brawn at 8 with a d12. Worst: the exit roll, 1 + 5 with Good Dog and Fetch on a 12.5% Trouble, with the essential lock in his paws. Then the gamble.
- **Downtime: 4 Turns** (Turn 3 lost to a Cost, then Turns 6–8 at the gate).
- Understood? Completely. He made most of the rules lookups and did the furniture Suspicion sums aloud. His Turn-9 gamble (risking the Limit on purpose to end a chase he was losing) was legal and split the table.
- Felt like a Werewolf? Fetch ("knows the way home") and Good Dog did. He never ran on all fours until the flight.

**Hoarder: Mummy** (Old Curse, Keeper of Treasures, Librarian)
- Best: a 19 in the last round of the flight, the best roll of the night, in the round they were forked. Worst: keeping his last charge through rounds 4 and 5 at Lead 2 "in case it gets worse". It got worse.
- **Downtime: 4 Turns** (6–9).
- Understood? Yes. He played the persona exactly: no charge spent until the final flight, the loud way rather than a risky watched roll, then all three in the flight, the last one only in round 7.
- Felt like a Mummy? Yes. His Tell (dust and spice) went off twice, at the printer and at the lock-up, and Ancient Lore's Wits d12 is the Mummy's whole personality. Keeper of Treasures never came up.

**Roleplayer: Jekyll & Hyde** (Pillar of Society, Practised Hand, Tailor)
- Best: the 8 + 8 Critical jailbreak, and the Monster turning the doctor into Hyde in the middle of the crowd. Worst: captured in one roll on Turn 3, and stuck as Jekyll on a Nimble ground in round 6 of the flight, with no charge to change and no overdraw allowed.
- **Downtime: 0 Turns.** He always had a roll, even in the lock-up. He got the most play of anyone.
- Understood? The Draught, Practised Hand and "Hyde takes over" took two lookups. He picked the fun option (the doorman, bending the bars) and twice the Planner talked him round to the quiet one.
- Felt like Jekyll & Hyde? The most of anyone. Hyde kept bursting out at the worst moments, and the table loved it.

- That is inside the book's "2–3 hours" for a raid that ended on Turn 9. A raid that ran to dawn would go past three hours at this pace.
- **The final flight alone is about 45–60 minutes by the formula** (7 rounds × 6 rolls). At a real table five people roll at once, so it might be 25–30 minutes, but it's still the longest single stretch of the night.
- **Long waits:**
  - The Witch's solo chase on Turn 2: 12 rolls, about 12 minutes, with four players watching.
  - Turns 6–8: the Witch, Werewolf and Mummy only waited for three Turns while Hyde and the Ghost tried for the armour, and the Mummy and the Witch also waited on Turn 9.
  - The Werewolf's lost Turn 3.

## 6. The story

On Lantern Night in Gallowsmere the bells rang every hour, and five monsters walked in through the gate. The Werewolf fell into Granny Mott's compost heap climbing the smithy wall. The Witch was so busy staring down the silversmith's guard dog that a gang of children spotted the cat. She ran across the rooftops for what felt like an hour, lost them in the back alleys, came straight back, and opened the strongbox. The Mummy shouted "Your cat's on the roof!" at the printer's nosy neighbour and simply walked in. Dr Jekyll talked his way past the tailor's doorman, had a funny turn in the crowded shop, and came out as Hyde. He was caught in the first alley he tried.

By Turn 4 the castle's whole year was in their hands: the new lock for the dungeon, the silver spoons, the black-edged writing paper, and the bandages, which the Ghost had drifted in and taken through a crowd that never noticed. Hyde slipped out of the lock-up with one perfect wriggle. Then the Ghost said the words "suit of armour". Three Turns later the cellar trapdoor was still shut and Gallowsmere was muttering.

At the gate, with everything in their arms, the Werewolf tripped over the threshold in front of Granny Mott. The bells started. Alone and losing, he let the wolf out on purpose, and the whole town came. All five ran together. Twice they were three streets from the woods. The Mummy did the best running of his three thousand years. But the market stalls went over the wrong way twice, and somebody had brought rowan, and horseshoes, and the hounds; a child grabbed a loose bandage end, and somebody in the crowd recognised the good doctor. The town still tells the story. Next Lantern Night, the bells will ring a little louder.
