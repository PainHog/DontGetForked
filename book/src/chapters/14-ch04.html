Chapter 4

The Raid

The raid happens on the town’s yearly festival, Lantern Night. Everyone dresses up as a monster, so the real ones can almost pass in the crowd until dawn ends the raid.

The Shopping List

Before the raid, the Storyteller rolls the shopping list on Chapter 8’s tables; Chapter 9’s towns come with their own. It has 4 items on Easy, 5 on Standard and Hard. One or two are essentials (one on Easy, one or two on Standard, two on Hard), and the rest are extras. To win, bring home every essential, with at most one extra missing. Each item is one of six kinds (the “Shops for” column of Chapter 2’s Castle Duties table), and its kind decides whose Castle Duty gives an edge at its location.

The Town

Every town is built from the same parts.

  • An obstacle lists the traits that work (one may be the loud way) and has a Difficulty. It may be marked watched (Trouble there gets you caught) and group (below).
  • A location has 1–3 obstacles, which you get past in order, guarding one list item (sometimes two, when two items share a place). Whoever gets past the last obstacle has the item in hand, with no extra action. If several get past at once, they choose who holds it.
  • A location’s first obstacle comes in two versions, its two ways in (say, front door or back window). The party picks one the first time anyone rolls at one of them, even with an approach of its own and even if the roll fails, and everyone uses only that one there for the rest of the raid. (A Ghost’s Spectral pass, with no roll, doesn’t pick it.)
  • A location is watched if any of its obstacles is, either way in included (the furniture’s doesn’t count). It gets a Tell check. Being caught depends only on the obstacle you roll.
  • The way out of town (below) is always watched.
  • The lock-up, where captives are held, is always watched too.

A place is any location, the way out or the lock-up. An item is a thing on the shopping list, and the items an Entity carries are its loot. The furniture or decor is a piece (below).

Turns

The night lasts 12 Turns. The party starts at the edge of town, at the way out, and entering the town needs no roll. Each Turn, every Entity takes one action:

  • Roll at the next obstacle where it is.
  • Move to any other place. A move takes one Turn, unless a map says it takes two. Carrying furniture adds one Turn to any move.
  • Pick up dropped items (below).
  • Wait.

Entities act one at a time, in any order, and each result counts at once. Finish one Entity’s action, chase and all, before the next begins; if the hunt starts mid-Turn, anyone who hasn’t acted yet doesn’t. An Entity who moves has arrived, and an obstacle one Entity beats is already beaten for the next to act. Several may try the same obstacle in one Turn, and each try risks Trouble, though the way out can be rolled only once a Turn. The party may split up, and it costs no extra Tells, only earlier ones (Chapter 5), though the Witch’s Familiar’s Warning covers only those who arrive with her. On Standard and Hard it usually should, since there’s rarely time to visit every place together. Dawn comes when the 12th Turn ends, and anyone still in town runs for it in the final flight.

Once anyone beats an obstacle (a Success or a Cost), it stays beaten for the whole party, with two exceptions.

  • At a group obstacle (a crowd, say, or a guard who checks everyone), each Entity who wants past rolls for itself.
  • If someone got past an obstacle with its own approach, the others must still beat it.

If two or more Entities roll the same group obstacle in one Turn, they make one group check instead of acting one at a time. All declare their dice and abilities, then all roll at once, and each gets past on its own result. Suspicion rises only once for the whole check, by the biggest trigger among the rolls.

Furniture

One piece of furniture or decor stands at one of the list’s locations, behind one extra obstacle of its own, after the location’s others. That obstacle’s Difficulty is 2 higher than it would otherwise be (at most 12). Once that location’s loot has been taken, whoever is past all its other obstacles may try for the piece. Bringing it home turns a Win into a Grand Year.

Carrying

  • A Bulky piece needs one carrier and a Huge piece two; Chapter 8’s furniture table says which is which. A Huge piece’s two carriers both count as carrying it, move together, and lose it if either is captured. A Huge piece can’t go through a way in or way out that a map marks small.
  • While you carry a piece, you can’t use the Mask, and your Nimble is one size smaller. Carrying loot doesn’t do this.
  • When you carry a piece, a move takes your action in one more Turn than usual: two Turns in a row for an ordinary move. Until the last, you’re between places, at neither one. You arrive when you take your action in the last.
  • Taking the piece, once you’re past its obstacle, is free, then or on a later Turn. Taking it back up after it’s been set down is an action.
  • From the Turn it’s first taken, it raises Suspicion by 1 at the end of each Turn, even while set down. That stops when it’s carried out of town, lost (the town takes it back when its carrier is captured) or abandoned.
  • You may abandon it at any time, free. It stays where it is and makes no more noise, and nobody can take it again.

Loot. An Entity can carry any amount of loot, and can hand it to another Entity in the same place at any time, free. It can’t while it’s caught, until its chase ends, and it can never hand loot to a captive (though a captive who kept its loot can hand it out).

Dropping and Picking Up

  • You may drop your own loot, or a piece you carry, at any time without using your action. Once you’re caught, though, you can’t until your chase ends.
  • It stays at the place you’re in. Between places, it stays at the place you left, and setting down a piece there puts you back at that place too.
  • Anyone at that place may pick up any number of dropped items, as their action for a Turn.

Getting Out

The way out is one watched obstacle: Sly or Nimble, or Brawn the loud way, at Difficulty 6 on Easy, 8 on Standard and 10 on Hard. The party leaves together. One Entity rolls for everyone, but only when every Entity who isn’t captured is at the way out. A Success or a Cost gets everyone out, and a Cost here costs nothing. On Trouble, Suspicion rises and the roller is caught. The way out can be rolled once a Turn, so try again next Turn. Anyone still captured is left behind.
