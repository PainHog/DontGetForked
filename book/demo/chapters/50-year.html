Part Three

How the Year Went

About 5–10 minutes. An item, or the mirror, is home if an Entity who got out of town carries it, whether that Entity left by the way out or escaped the final flight. Find the result, then read its line.

Read the result’s line aloud
Grand YearA Win, and the mirror came home too.A year of plenty. The new piece goes in the great hall, and everyone pretends it was always there.
WinThe wine came home, and no more than one of the other three items is missing.Full larders, warm fires. The castle creaks happily through another year.
PartialNot a Win, but at least 2 of the 4 items came home. Without the wine, a Partial at best.The castle gets by, just. Someone has to sleep in the draughty tower.
Bust0 or 1 of the 4 items came home.A long, thin year, and a lot of arguing about whose fault it was.
ForkedCornered in the final flight, or every Entity in the lock-up at once.The town tells the story for years. Next Lantern Night, the bells ring a little louder.

Left behind: work out the result first, then drop it one step for each Entity left in the lock-up, so a Grand Year becomes a Win, a Win a Partial, a Partial a Bust. It never drops below Bust.

Then, unless the result is Forked, read one line for each item that didn’t come home. If the nightshade is one of them, end the goodnight’s first sentence at “spoons”.

Read the line aloud
The wine (food and drink)Turnip soup every night until spring.
The nightshade (plants and seeds)Nothing grows but nettles, and the nettles win.
The hinges (tools and hardware)Every door in the castle creaks, sticks or falls off.
The cape (cloth and costumes)Next year’s disguises are held together with string and hope.

Goodnight

And that was Lantern Night in Hallowby. Somewhere down the hill, a town is counting its spoons, and a florist is wondering where the nightshade went.

Happy Halloween.
