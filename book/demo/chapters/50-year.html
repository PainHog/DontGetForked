Part Three

How the Year Went

About 5–10 minutes. An item, or the mirror, is home if an Entity who got out of town carries it, whether that Entity left by the way out or escaped the final flight. Find the result, then read its line.

Read the result’s line aloud
Grand YearA Win, and the mirror came home too.A year of plenty, and the new piece takes pride of place in the great hall.
WinThe wine came home, and no more than one of the other three items is missing.The pantry is full and the fires stay lit all year.
PartialNot a Win, but at least 2 of the 4 items came home. Without the wine, a Partial at best.The castle gets by, but barely, and the tower stays cold all winter.
Bust0 or 1 of the 4 items came home.A long, hard year, with too little to go around.
ForkedCornered in the final flight, or every Entity in the lock-up at once.The town tells the story for years, and next Lantern Night it keeps a closer watch.

Left behind: work out the result first, then drop it one step for each Entity left in the lock-up, so a Grand Year becomes a Win, a Win a Partial, a Partial a Bust. It never drops below Bust.

Then, unless the result is Forked, read one line for each item that didn’t come home. If the nightshade didn’t come home, or the year is Forked, leave out the part about the florist.

Read the line aloud
The wine (food and drink)Turnip soup every night until spring.
The nightshade (plants and seeds)Nothing grows in the garden but nettles.
The hinges (tools and hardware)Doors stick, hinges break and nothing gets fixed.
The cape (cloth and costumes)Next year’s disguises are old and patched together with string.

Goodnight

And that was Lantern Night in Hallowby. Down the hill, the town is waking up to find things missing, and the florist is wondering where her nightshade went.

Happy Halloween.
