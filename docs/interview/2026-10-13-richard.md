# Richard's interview, 2026-10-13

Richard Moore's own answers, word for word as he typed them, for the rulebook's introduction and designer's notes. Only spelling and punctuation may be touched when they are placed, and Richard approves each placement.

## 1. Where did the idea come from?
I was trying to think of a halloween tabletop game, my table runs something every year. We always aim for a one shot, but it sometimes takes us a few weeks anyways. I came up with the idea of monsters having to restock their supplies, and the name just came to me. Pitchforks and it sounded funny.

## 2. Why classic monsters, and why these eight? A favourite?
They are easily referenced historical figures that gave diversity to the game. I didnt want to fabricate a ton of characters for this. I actually favor Jekyll & Hyde.

## 3. Why a shopping trip?
I mean monsters needing supplies and thats how the villagers catch them. I thought it just fit the idea of being chased away, like robbers get chased. But what makes it funny is being monsters, trying not to be seen as monsters, stealing things for your monster castle.

## 4. The Mask or the Monster
Monsters generally dont want to be found out as monsters, thats how villages chase you down and kill you or burn your castle down. Sometimes you need to be the monster though, thats the point of being a monster! But they are living in a Human world.

## 5. What Heisty Spideys taught
im not sure this is relevant. However, It taught me how to structure a game that players enjoyed and test challenges and decision making during conceptual play and testing

## 6. A group's first night
Chaos, Mishaps, Running with loot, Excitement

## 8. The Storyteller
This is not a game where the ST is trying to beat the players, you are creating room for chaos and letting the chaos take the table in various directions that will result in fun outcomes. The players dying is the fate of the dice, not the objective for running the game.
