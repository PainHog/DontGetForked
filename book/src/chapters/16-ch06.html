Chapter 6

Chases and Getting Forked

There are two kinds of chase. Get caught during the raid and you run from a crowd; botch the whole job and the entire town turns out with pitchforks.

The Lead

Every chase uses a Lead track. Each round you roll against the mob’s Difficulty:

  • Success: Lead +1 (a Critical: +2).
  • Cost: no change.
  • Trouble: Lead −1.

Reach the escape number and you are clear. Reach 0 and you are cornered. In a chase the roll moves the Lead: a Cost costs nothing more, and Trouble doesn’t start another chase. In a local chase, Trouble and the Monster showing still raise Suspicion (one roll, one rise).

The Ground

Each round, roll a d6 on the chase table at the end of this chapter. The result says which traits work this round for everyone in the chase, and always includes one that isn’t Nimble.

Your Weakness

Once the mob brings your Weakness, you roll your trait one size smaller for the rest of the chase. Each Weakness (Chapter 2) says when the mob brings it:

  • Always: from the first round of any chase.
  • Soon: from the third round of any chase.
  • Dawn: only in a final flight that dawn started, from its first round.

The Local Chase

When you are caught during the raid, you run. Your Lead starts at 1 and you escape at 4. The mob’s Difficulty is 10 plus half the Suspicion (round down), at most 12, checked each round. You may use the Mask. In a local chase, abilities help only your own roll. The chase happens within the Turn it started; escape and you’re back where you were caught, with your Turn used up. Entities caught together in one group check flee together on one shared Lead, which moves by the majority rule below, and are captured together if cornered.

Captured

Cornered in a local chase, you are captured, and the town takes back what you were carrying: it’s gone for the night. You are held at the town’s lock-up.

  • Rescue: the lock-up is an obstacle — Sly, or Brawn the loud way, always watched — at the lock-up Difficulty, with a new rescue obstacle for each capture. Getting there costs the party Turns. Beat it (a Success or a Cost) and every captive there is free and acts again next Turn; a rescuer’s Trouble gets them caught as usual.
  • Slipping free: once per Turn, from the Turn after its capture, a captive may try to slip free: Sly or Nimble, or Brawn the loud way, at the lock-up Difficulty. Only a Success frees you; a Cost does nothing, and on Trouble Suspicion rises but no chase starts. A freed Entity acts again next Turn.
  • Left behind: anyone still held when the party leaves town is left behind (Chapter 7).

The Final Flight

At the Limit, or at dawn, every Entity who isn’t captured flees together. The Lead starts at 2 and the party escapes at 6. The mob’s Difficulty is set by the town’s difficulty (Chapter 8) and doesn’t change with the size of the party. Everyone flees together, so abilities can help anyone’s roll.

The Majority Rule

Everyone rolls each round. If Successes outnumber Trouble, the Lead rises by 1; if Trouble outnumbers Successes, it falls by 1; otherwise it stays. A Critical counts as two Successes.

Escape and you are home with the goods. Get cornered and the party is forked: the monsters are killed and the raid is lost.

Once the Hunt Is On

From the moment the final flight begins, Suspicion stops and the Mask is off: everyone rolls the Monster die. You may still overdraw, but your Weakness is then in play from your next roll to the end of the flight, and while your Weakness is in play, however it came, you can’t overdraw.
