# Entity tuning after the outside review: measurements (2026-10-20)

A measuring job for Richard's calls on the outside review's Section 4 ("Entity tuning") and on Thistlewick. **Nothing in the game, the book, the Foundry system or `module/config.mjs` was changed.** Each option is a switch the simulator reads only when `sim/tuning-check.mjs` sets it (`sim/params.mjs` TUNING, kept out of PARAMS so `run.mjs` doesn't sweep it). With the switches off the simulator plays exactly as before: a fingerprint of default raids (rolled towns under three player styles, the three premade towns and Hallowby) was identical before and after the change, and `npm run check` passes (330 tests).

Everything is at today's defaults (the review's package, Suspicion Limits 9 / 10 / 13, 12 Turns), on seeds 1, 7 and 11, with common random numbers: each option replays its baseline's parties, towns and dice. Rolled towns: 2,000 raids per label and party size per seed (54,000 a row). One Entity's win rate: that Entity in every party of 4, 4,000 raids per label per seed (36,000 a row). Towns: 3,000 raids per party size per seed (27,000 a row). How it was run is in "How it was measured" below.

**Two kinds of party.** *Random kits* is the simulator's standard: each Entity's Gift and Perk at random. Every target so far was measured on it, and changing which option is the default does nothing there. *Default kits* gives every Entity its marked default Gift, Perk and Castle Duty. That is what a first-time table and the one-shot's Entity cards play, so default changes are measured on it, and Hallowby is measured on both.

## Summary and recommendations

**The baseline.** Every level is on target on both kinds of party (random kits: 92.4 / 76.0 / 55.4% won, 1.0 / 3.4 / 9.5% forked; default kits: 92.8 / 75.5 / 55.9%, 0.9 / 3.5 / 10.9%). Hallowby is 90.8% / 1.6% on random kits and 90.9% / 1.8% on default kits. In a party of 4, the Entities' Standard / Hard wins run from 76.0 / 54.6% (Jekyll & Hyde, the Creature) to 78.8 / 58.6% (A Ghost) on random kits. On default kits the spread is wider: 75.4 / 54.2% (Jekyll & Hyde) to 78.7 / 59.3% (the Invisible Man). On default kits, three Entities are outside the ±2.5 with-vs-without target **today**: Jekyll & Hyde −3.5, the Invisible Man +3.1 and Dracula +2.9. **1. Dracula.** The review is right that Hypnotic Eyes barely matters. Players who know it exactly gain nothing (−0.3), and he takes the Monster on 40% of his Charm rolls with it. But he is already the strongest default kit (+2.9). D-a turns the Perk into his best one (the Monster die on every Charm roll, Mesmerise included) and makes him a +6 outlier. D-b changes nothing in the numbers. D-c is the only option that pulls him in, but it lifts default-kit Hard forked to 11.9% (12.2% on one seed). **Recommendation: no change for tomorrow.** If Richard wants the default Perk to come up more, Old Money (D-b) costs nothing in balance and is the easiest to teach: the player does nothing, and the Storyteller just never picks Suspicion as the Cost of his Charm rolls. "Beats your die by 2" needs a comparison on every Charm roll. "Never shows" (D-a) is the simplest of all to teach, and too strong.

**2. Wall-Crawler.** In the simulator, his lone local chases escape 43.3% of the time with Hypnotic Eyes and 43.8% with Wall-Crawler. His Weakness (Garlic, from round 1) makes the d10 a d8 in every chase, and his Bat Gift already switches to Nimble. Measured the same way, the review's yardstick (the Witch's Fly by Night) adds 5.1 points (50.2 → 55.3%), not 13–19. Fly by Night also costs her 1–2 points of wins against her default Familiar's Warning. **W-e is the second edge to take:** +7.2 points of chase escape, more than Fly by Night, moving the levels by at most 0.2 and Dracula's own wins by under 1 point. It is one sentence: "your Nimble is one size bigger in a chase". The table needs one note: it is the roll's one raise, so no ability raises it further in the final flight (in practice it cancels Garlic on his Nimble rolls). W-b does nothing. W-d adds 0.4 points and a second job for a chase Perk.

**3. The Creature.** C-a changes nothing measurable. The carrier almost never rolls, because one Entity rolls the way out for everyone and the final flight forces the Monster die anyway. Tireless and Built to Last are also within ±0.2 of Strong Back. The Creature is balanced on default kits (−0.5), so the choice is about teaching. **Recommendation: C-b (Tireless as the default)** over C-a. It is one plain sentence, already printed, and it works with the Bulky pieces every printed town has. C-a adds words to a Perk and still does nothing at the table. If Richard prefers a default that comes up in almost every raid, Built to Last ("you slip free on a Success or a Cost") is the printed one that does. A caveat on the model: the simulated party hands furniture to the smallest Nimble unless the Creature has Strong Back, so it never gives the piece to a Tireless Creature on purpose. Because carriers roll so rarely, that can't add more than a few tenths.

**4. The Mummy.** **Recommendation: M-a (Fear the Curse as the default).** It is the only Mummy Perk that bites (+0.6 / +1.3, ten more points of chase escape). It brings her from −0.4 to +0.5 on default kits. It is simple to teach: in your own chase, the mob is 1 easier.

**5. The Invisible Man.** **Recommendation: keep V17's wording.** I-a is +3.2 / +4.7 for the Entity already at +3.1 on default kits.

**6. The Witch.** **Recommendation: Wi-a (A Potion for That as the default).** It works like the Mummy's Ancient Lore and the Werewolf's Keen Nose ("roll Wits instead"), so a new player gets it at once. **7. A raise on a d12.** Today 45% of all rolls are made on a d12 trait die (53% at obstacles). ## Reproducing

```
node sim/tuning-check.mjs --runs 2000 --entity-runs 4000 --town-runs 3000 --seeds 1,7,11 --jobs 4 --save results.json --out sim/TUNING-2026-10-20.md
```

That command runs every option and candidate (573 jobs, about 50 minutes on 4 cores) and writes everything from "How it was measured" on. This summary was written from those numbers; pass it with `--summary <file>` to keep it on top. For the record, the run was made in parts (`--opts G-b`, `--opts FbN`, `--opts PKG,J-c`, `--opts PKG2`) and merged with `--merge a.json,b.json,…`. The numbers are the same either way, because every job is seeded on its own.

**The simulator changes** (all off by default; `sim/params.mjs` TUNING documents each one):
- `sim/engine.mjs`: hypnoticEyes "never", perkAware, wallCrawler carry / wayIn / bigger, strongBack "mask", raiseD12 plus2 / plus1, and tuneStats counters.
- `sim/rules.mjs`: a `showMin` option on the exact odds, so the players can price Hypnotic Eyes.
- `sim/entities.mjs` and `sim/run.mjs`: default kits, forcing one Entity into the party, overriding one Entity's kit, and a Gift version rolling another trait.
- New: `sim/tuning-check.mjs` and this report. `sim/README.md` lists the script.

## How it was measured

- **Rules and numbers:** today's defaults (the outside review's package D1–D7 with R1 and D4, Suspicion Limits 9 / 10 / 13, 12 Turns; `sim/params.mjs`).
- **Seeds:** 1, 7, 11. Every option runs on the same parties, towns and dice streams as its baseline (common random numbers), so a difference is the option, not a different draw. Run-to-run noise between seeds is about ±0.5–1 point on a level's win rate (more on one Entity's).
- **Rolled towns:** 2,000 raids per label and party size (3, 4 and 5 Entities) per seed, 18,000 raids a run.
- **One Entity's win rate (party of 4):** that Entity in every party of 4, the other three at random: the same parties as "raids that include it" in a rolled run, 4,000 per label per seed (36,000 per row).
- **Towns:** Hallowby (the one-shot), Puddlecombe, Thistlewick and Gallowsmere as printed, 3,000 raids per party size (3, 4, 5) per seed.
- **Kits.** *Random kits* is the simulator's standard: each Entity's Gift and Perk at random (as Chapter 2's d6 would), a Castle Duty at random; every target so far was measured on it. Changing which option is the **default** changes nothing there. *Default kits* gives every Entity its marked default Gift, Perk and Castle Duty (if two defaults clash, the later one takes another at random): what a first-time table plays, and what the one-shot's Entity cards have (Hallowby is measured on both).
- **Hypnotic Eyes and the players.** The simulated players price the Monster die from exact odds. Before this job they priced Dracula's Charm Monster rolls as if Hypnotic Eyes didn't exist (the Perk still worked when the dice were read). `aware` is the baseline with players who know it; option D-a is compared with both.
- ✗ marks a number outside its target. Δ is in points against today's defaults on the same seeds; the per-seed Δs show the noise.

```
node sim/tuning-check.mjs --runs 2000 --entity-runs 4000 --town-runs 3000 --seeds 1,7,11 --jobs 4
```
