Part Two

The Final Flight

When Suspicion reaches 11, or dawn comes with anyone still in town. Stop wherever you are, even in the middle of a Turn or a chase. If a local chase was running, it ends at once and its runners join the flight; anyone cornered in that same round is captured first. Captives stay in the lock-up.

That’s it. Hallowby has had enough. The bells are ringing and the fiddles have stopped. Every lantern in town is turning your way, and with them come pitchforks, rolling pins, the whole costume contest, and old Granny Mott at the front.

Run for the castle. All of you, together, right now.

This is the final flight. Everyone who isn’t locked up runs together, from wherever you are.

Your Lead starts at 2. Get it to 5 and you’re home. If it drops to 0, they’ve got you, and you’re forked.

The masks are off now. Everyone rolls the Monster, and Suspicion doesn’t matter any more. Every round I roll the ground, and it gives you two traits to choose from. Each of you rolls one of them against the mob, and you can spend charges on each other’s rolls.

Then we count. More Successes than Trouble, your Lead goes up by 1, or by 2 if you’re two or more ahead. More Trouble than Successes, it goes down the same way. Level, it stays. A Cost counts for neither, and a Critical counts as two Successes.

Anything you drop now stays in Hallowby.

Running It

  1. Who runs: every Entity who isn’t captured. The Lead starts at 2. Escape at 5. Cornered at 0 means Forked.
  2. The mob’s Difficulty is 10, however many run. Suspicion has stopped, and nothing raises it now.
  3. Each round: roll a d6 on the ground table (When It Happens) and read its line. Each runner picks one of its two traits. The Monster d10 only. Carrying the mirror makes Nimble one size smaller. Abilities may go on anyone’s roll. Everyone rolls, in any order the party likes.
  4. The majority rule: count the Successes (a Critical counts as two) and the Trouble; Costs count for neither. If Successes are ahead by 1, the Lead goes up 1, and by 2 or more, it goes up 2. If Trouble is ahead, it goes down the same way. Level means no change. For example, a Success, a Critical, a Cost and a Trouble make 3 Successes to 1 Trouble, so the Lead goes up 2.
  5. The Weakness: the flight is a new chase for everyone, even someone who came straight from a local chase. From round 1 for Dracula and a Ghost, from round 3 for everyone else, the trait die is one size smaller to the end of the flight. Read its line. A bigger die from an ability cancels it for that roll.
  6. Overdraw now costs the Weakness instead of Suspicion. From the overdrawer’s own next roll to the end of the flight, its trait die is one size smaller. Once per flight each, and never while its Weakness is already in play. (So Dracula and a Ghost can’t overdraw in the flight.)

Home

The Lead reaches 5. Everyone in the flight is home, with everything they carry. Anyone still in the lock-up is left behind.

The last pitchfork falls behind in the woods, and the castle door slams shut. Somewhere far below, Hallowby is still shouting at the trees.

You made it. Let’s see what you brought home.

Forked

The Lead reaches 0. The year’s result is Forked, whatever the party carries. Turn to Part Three and read only the Forked line.

They’ve cornered you at the bottom of the hill, with the castle’s windows glowing just above the trees. The lanterns close in, and up come the pitchforks.

You got forked.
