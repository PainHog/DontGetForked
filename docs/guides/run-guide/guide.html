Running Lantern Night

Your guide for the night. Page numbers are the one-shot’s (Lantern Night in Hallowby, 32 pages). The whole night takes about 1½ to 2 hours.

Before the Game

  • Print the Entity cards (pages 28–31) and cut them out, and the tracker (page 32).
  • Print the player map (the separate one-page Hallowby map) for the middle of the table. It shows the places, but none of your Difficulties.
  • Dice. Each player needs a d4, d6, d8, d10 and d12, plus a second d6 (the Mask) and a second d10 (the Monster). Players can share. You need one d6.
  • Read pages 4–6 (Before You Play and Hallowby at a Glance). Everything after that you read aloud as you go.
  • Foundry: install or update to 0.1.6. On the setup screen, open Game Systems and click Update on Don’t Get Forked, or Install System with the manifest link. It carries today’s rules (the six-round chase, one overdraw a chase, the lower Limit with five players) and the new starting powers. If you install the zip by hand instead, delete the old system first.
  • Foundry: set up the one-shot. Open your world as the GM and click Set it up when it asks “Set up Lantern Night in Hallowby?”. That brings in the monsters, both scenes, the script and the macro. Missed it? Configure Settings has a Set up the one-shot button. (By hand: the Compendium tab, the Demo folder, right-click each pack, Import All Content, the script first, keeping the IDs.)
  • Foundry: a 15-minute dry run with a second browser window logged in as a player. Give ownership, run the macro, make one roll and click Next Turn.
  • Then, if you like, make the repository private. Do it after you’ve updated to 0.1.6: once it’s private, Foundry can’t update by itself, and later versions come as a zip to install by hand.

The Maps in Foundry

Nothing to make. The Demo has two scenes, ready to use.

  • The Castle is a moonlit castle for Part One. Its one pin opens Part One of the script.
  • Hallowby is the night map of the town: the four shops, the lock-up and the bridge out (The Way Out), each with a yard to stand tokens in. There’s no grid and no fog. Where a token stands only says which place it’s at, and every move takes one Turn, wherever it goes.
  • The pins are yours alone. Players don’t see them. Click one to open that place’s page of the script.
  • Players move their own tokens once they own their Entity.

On the Night

Part One: The Castle · 20–25 minutes · pages 7–10

Show The Castle scene and read the boxes in order. You welcome them, read the shopping list and introduce the eight monsters, and each player picks one. Then come their card, a practice roll, powers, Castle Duties and setting off.

In Foundry, once everyone has chosen:

  1. Give each player Owner of their Entity. Right-click it in the Actors list and choose Configure Ownership.
  2. Change Castle Duties on the sheets if anyone swapped.
  3. Run the macro Hallowby: Start the Raid. Drag it to the hotbar and click it. The Raid window shows an Easy raid, Suspicion 0 of 9 (8 with five players), Turn 1 and the shopping list. It has to come after step 1, because the raid takes every Entity a player owns.
  4. Switch to the Hallowby scene and drag each Entity onto the yard at The Way Out.

This is the natural place for a break.

Part Two: Into Hallowby · 45–90 minutes · pages 11–24

Read The Edge of Town and How the Night Works (pages 11–12), and walk them through their first roll with the box on page 12. Then run every Turn the same way. The loop is on the tracker too.

  1. Say the Turn, as in “Turn 3.”
  2. Go round the table. Ask, “What does your monster do?” Each does one thing: roll, move, pick up, or wait.
  3. A move: turn to that place (pages 13–18) and read its arrival box. The first time anyone reaches a watched place, make a Tell check (page 20).
  4. A roll: ask which trait, Mask or Monster, and whether anyone spends a charge. They roll, and you read the result line on the place’s page.
  5. End of the Turn: if the mirror has been taken and is still in town, Suspicion +1. Move the Turn marker. In Foundry, click Next Turn.

The Final Flight · pages 25–26

When Suspicion reaches 9 (8 with five players), or Turn 12 ends with anyone still in town, stop wherever you are and turn to page 25. The whole party runs together from the mob.

Part Three: How the Year Went · 5–10 minutes · page 27

Work out what came home, then read the result’s line, a line for each item that didn’t make it, and the goodnight.

The Rules Most Likely to Trip You Up

  • One roll raises Suspicion only once, by its biggest trigger. The exception is an overdraw (using an ability with no charges left): its +2 always goes on top.
  • A chase raises Suspicion only once, when it ends: +2 if the Monster showed on any round, +1 if there was any Trouble. The mob’s Difficulty is set when the chase starts and doesn’t change. A runner can give up at the start of any round, once the ground is read and before anyone rolls, and go to the lock-up. After round 6, a Lead of 2 or more gets away and less is cornered.
  • In any chase, any ability can be spent as a raise (trait die one size bigger), whatever it usually does (in a local chase, on the runner’s own roll).
  • Everyone in the lock-up at once ends the night, and the year is Forked.
  • The Monster shows when its d10 is higher than the trait die: Suspicion +2, even on a Success. A tie doesn’t count, and the Mask never shows.
  • Trouble at a watched obstacle means caught, and a chase, unless it takes Suspicion to the Limit: then it’s the final flight for everyone. Where nobody’s watching, it’s just Suspicion +1 and they can try again next Turn.
  • Tell check: the first time anyone arrives at a watched place, roll a d6. On 4–6 a Tell goes off, Suspicion +1.
  • The mirror adds Suspicion +1 at the end of every Turn once it’s taken, until it’s out of town.
  • Leaving town: everyone who isn’t locked up has to be at the bridge, and then one of them rolls for all, at Difficulty 6.
  • Not sure? Make a quick ruling and keep the night moving. The script says the same.

Playing at a Table Instead

Everything above works without Foundry. Players use their printed cards, you keep Suspicion, the Turns and the Lead on the printed tracker with a coin on each track, and players move a coin or a token on the player map to show where their monster is.
