Part Two

When It Happens

A Cost

The roller does it, but you pick one cost. Never pick one that costs nothing right then: not Suspicion +1 on a roll that already raised Suspicion, not “drop an item” from someone carrying nothing, not a lost Turn when nothing is waiting. Check the roller’s Perk: the Mummy never loses a Turn as its Cost.

Read the line aloud
Suspicion +1You do it, but somebody noticed something odd about you. Suspicion goes up by 1.
Drop an itemYou do it, but in the scramble you drop something. It’s lying right here.
Lose a TurnYou do it, but it takes forever. You miss your next go.
Smaller dieYou do it, but you’ve twisted something. Your next roll’s trait die is one size smaller.

Drop an item: one item of loot the roller carries, which you pick: never what this roll wins, never the mirror. It stays in this place, and picking up dropped items is an action. Mix the Costs up; a dropped item is the most fun, because someone has to come back for it.

Trouble

It doesn’t happen, and Suspicion +1. Read the obstacle’s Trouble line. If the obstacle is watched, the roller is caught: turn to Caught. If not, that’s all: they can try again next Turn.

The Monster Shows

The Monster d10 rolled higher than the trait die: Suspicion +2, whatever the result. Not on a tie, and never with the Mask. If an ability let them roll the Monster without risking Suspicion (the Werewolf’s Good Dog, the Invisible Man’s Unseen, some Gifts), its showing adds nothing. Some Perks change this: Dracula’s Hypnotic Eyes, on Charm rolls. When the Monster shows on one of Jekyll’s rolls, the roll stands, and then Hyde takes over, free.

The CreatureIt straightens to its full height, and keeps on straightening.
The MummyA bandage comes loose, and keeps on unwinding, and there is nobody inside.

A Critical

A Success where both dice show the same number. Outside a chase, the roller gets back one charge it spent (never more than 3). In a chase, it counts as two Successes.

Perfect. Not a sound, not a witness, and you feel a little of your power come back.

A Tell Check

The first time anyone reaches a watched place: the florist, the carpenter, the tailor, the lock-up (not a captive’s arrival) and the way out once anyone comes back to it. Each place is checked once a night. Wait until everyone moving there this Turn has arrived, then roll a d6. On 4–6, a Tell goes off: number the arrivals, roll to pick one, and read its line. Suspicion +1; that’s its only effect. The Witch’s Familiar’s Warning: if she is among the arrivals, a Tell goes off only if a second d6 also rolls 4–6.

Read the line aloud
DraculaA dark shop window reflects the whole street: the lanterns, the crowd, the grinning turnips. Everyone but you. Someone looks from the window, to you, and back.
The CreatureYou stand a head above the whole crowd, and no costume in the world hides it. A child on her father’s shoulders looks you right in the eye.
The MummyYou leave a trail of dust behind you, and a smell of old spices. A woman sniffs, frowns, and follows it with her eyes, all the way to you.
The WerewolfYour eyebrows meet in the middle, and there’s a little too much hair everywhere else. A man nudges his friend. “Good costume,” the friend says. “Very good.”
The Invisible ManBandages, goggles, a false nose, and then a sneeze, from nowhere at all. Three people say “Bless you” and look round for whoever sneezed.
A GhostThe candles gutter as you drift in, and everybody’s breath fogs. Someone pulls their shawl tighter and peers into the cold.
A WitchYour black cat sits down in the middle of the floor and stares at people. Unblinking. One by one, they stare back.
Jekyll & HydeOne of your hands is a gentleman’s. The other is hairy and knotted. Somebody shaking it notices.

A Group Check

When two or more roll the florist’s crowded shop floor in the same Turn. Everyone declares their trait, their die and any charges first, then all roll at once. Each gets past on its own result. Suspicion rises only once for the whole check, by the biggest single trigger among the rolls. Everyone who gets Trouble is caught together, and flees together on one Lead (Caught, below).

You go into the crowd together. Ready? Everybody roll at once.

Caught: the Local Chase

Trouble at a watched obstacle. The whole chase, however many rounds, happens inside this Turn. While it lasts, the runner can’t drop or hand over anything.

They’ve seen you. Somebody shouts. Somebody else grabs a broom. Run!

This is a chase. Your Lead starts at 1. Get it to 4 and you’ve lost them. If it drops to 0, they’ve cornered you.

Every round, I’ll tell you what the ground is like, and it gives you two traits to choose from. Roll one of them, with the Mask or the Monster, against the mob. A Success puts your Lead up by 1. Trouble puts it down by 1. A Cost leaves it where it is.

  1. The Lead starts at 1. The Werewolf’s Night Runner, fleeing alone: Lead 2.
  2. The mob’s Difficulty is 8 plus half the Suspicion, rounded down, at most 12. Work it out again at the start of every round. Suspicion 0–1: 8 · 2–3: 9 · 4–5: 10 · 6–7: 11 · 8–10: 12.
  3. Each round, roll a d6 on the ground table below and read the line. The runner picks one of its two traits, and the Mask or the Monster (no Mask carrying the mirror). Abilities go on the runner’s own roll only: nobody else can spend a charge on it. No Castle Duty in a chase, and no opening a way of your own.
  4. Move the Lead: a Success +1 (a Critical +2), a Cost no change, Trouble −1.
  5. Suspicion still rises in a local chase: Trouble +1, the Monster showing +2, an overdraw +2, once per roll, by its biggest trigger.
  6. The Weakness: from round 1 for Dracula and a Ghost, from round 3 for everyone else, the runner’s trait die is one size smaller to the end of the chase. Read its line when it starts.
  7. Escape at 4: they’re back where they were caught, with this Turn used up. Cornered at 0: captured (below).

Caught together (Trouble in the same group check): everyone in it rolls each round, in any order they like, and the Lead moves once for the round by the majority rule (The Final Flight). Suspicion rises once a round, by the biggest trigger. A Perk that changes a local chase only works for someone fleeing alone. If Suspicion reaches 11 during the chase, it ends at once and the runners join the final flight; anyone cornered that same round is captured first.

Captured

Cornered in a local chase. The town takes back everything the captive carried, the mirror included: it’s gone for the night, and nobody can take it again. Put the captive’s token in the lock-up.

Hands grab you from every side. Somebody sits on you. When you get your breath back, you’re in the lock-up, and the constable is very pleased with himself. Everything you were carrying is gone.

From next Turn, once a Turn, you can try to slip free. Or the others can come and get you. Anyone still in here when they leave town is left behind.

A captive can’t pick anything up or be handed anything, but can still spend charges on any roll at the lock-up: its own, a rescuer’s, or another captive’s.

Rescue and Slipping Free

A rescue watched

Anyone not held, at the lock-up (getting there is a move). Sly to sneak past the constable, or Brawn, the loud way: tear the door off. Difficulty 10. Watched.

Success or Cost: every captive there is free (pick a Cost as usual). The freed act again next Turn, from the lock-up.

The door swings open. The constable snores on.

Trouble: Suspicion +1, and the rescuer is caught: a local chase.

The constable’s hat falls off. He isn’t asleep after all.

Slipping free watched

The captive, once a Turn, from the Turn after its capture. Sly or Nimble, or Brawn, the loud way. Difficulty 10. Watched.

Success: free, and acts again next Turn, from the lock-up. A Cost: nothing happens. Trouble: Suspicion +1, but no chase.

The bars, it turns out, are only a suggestion.

The lock-up is watched, so Dracula’s Mesmerise works on both. A rescue frees only those held at that moment.

Dropping, Picking Up and Handing Over

An Entity may drop its own loot, or the mirror it carries, any time, free, except while it’s caught (until its chase ends). Dropped things stay in that place; dropped between places, they stay at the place it left, and an Entity that sets the mirror down there is back at that place too. Anyone there may pick up any number of dropped items, as their action for a Turn. Loot can be handed to anyone in the same place, free, any time: not while caught, and never to a captive.

Suspicion Rising

Flavour only. Read each line the first time Suspicion reaches that number, so the players feel the town waking up.

Read the line aloud
4Somewhere a dog starts barking, and doesn’t stop.
7The church bell starts ringing, though it isn’t the hour. In the square, someone says, quite loudly, “Those aren’t costumes.”
9Pitchforks are coming out of sheds. Nobody is saying “for the parade” any more.
10Lanterns are gathering in the square. One more thing, just one, and they’ll come for you.

Overdraw

At zero charges an Entity can still use an ability: it costs Suspicion +2 instead (part of that roll’s one rise). In the final flight it costs the Weakness instead (The Final Flight).

The End of a Turn, and Dawn

At the end of every Turn: if the mirror has been taken and is still in town (carried or set down, not lost or abandoned), Suspicion +1. Then move the Turn marker.

Read the line aloud
Start of Turn 10Over the woods, the sky is turning grey. The fiddles are slowing down.
Start of Turn 12This is the last Turn before dawn. The first birds are singing. Anyone still in town when the sun comes up will have to run for it.
End of Turn 12The sun comes up over Hallowby, and every costume in the street turns back into a person. Except yours.

After the end of Turn 12, everyone still in town who isn’t locked up: The Final Flight.
