Chapter 3

Rolling the Dice

When the outcome is in doubt, you roll two dice — your trait die and your Mask or Monster die — and add them together.

The Roll

  1. The obstacle sets the trait. Each obstacle lists one or two traits that work, usually one of them the loud way (Suspicion +1 whatever the result, Chapter 5). Using any other trait needs an ability.
  2. Choose the second die: the Mask d6 or the Monster d10.
  3. Roll both, add them, and compare the total with the Difficulty: 6 easy, 8 standard, 10 hard, 12 daunting.

Results

  • Success: the total meets the Difficulty. You do it.
  • Cost: 1–2 short. You do it, at a cost the Storyteller picks: Suspicion +1, drop an item, lose a Turn (you skip your next action), or your next roll’s trait die one size smaller. A dropped item falls where you are; picking it up costs your next action. The Storyteller never picks a Cost that costs nothing right then (say, a Suspicion +1 the roll already raised); “drop an item” drops one of your own items, never what this roll wins.
  • Trouble: 3 or more short. It doesn’t happen, Suspicion rises by 1, and if anyone is watching, you are caught (Chapter 6).
  • Critical: a Success where both dice show the same face. In a chase it counts as two Successes; anywhere else you get back one spent charge, never above your starting number.

The Monster Shows

If you rolled the Monster die and it came up higher than your trait die, the monster did the work and it showed: Suspicion +2. On a tie, or with the Mask, nothing shows.

Abilities

Using an ability costs one charge. At zero charges you can still use one, at a price: overdraw costs Suspicion +2, or your Weakness once the hunt is on (Chapter 6). Every ability does one of four things:

  • Raise your trait die one size (d4 → d6 → d8 → d10 → d12; a d12 can’t go higher).
  • Use the ability’s trait instead of the one the obstacle calls for.
  • Roll the Monster die without risking Suspicion: if it shows, that raises nothing (Trouble or a Cost still counts).
  • Open an approach nobody else can take: if the obstacle doesn’t list the ability’s trait, roll that trait at 2 lower Difficulty. Never in a chase. Only you get through, though the way out and a rescue work as usual, and a captive may open its own way out. You are watched as usual.

Spend abilities before you roll. An ability can help any Entity’s roll at the same place, even past obstacles you haven’t crossed; helping costs the charge, not your action. The roller rolls its own die; you can only open an approach on your own roll. Several abilities may go on one roll, but it takes at most one raise, from any source, Castle Duty included. Raises and steps down cancel out (a d4 stepped down and raised stays a d4). Nothing makes a die smaller than a d4: further steps down are lost.
