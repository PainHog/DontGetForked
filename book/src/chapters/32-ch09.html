Chapter 9

Three Towns

Three towns ready to raid, one each for Easy, Standard and Hard, each with a map and everything needed to run it.

Each location’s rows run in order: its two ways in (picked the first time anyone tries the location, then kept for the raid), then each “Then”. The furniture’s obstacle is already 2 harder. It comes last, marked “for the piece”.

Reading the Town Pages

  • Watched: Trouble there gets you caught. (group): each Entity rolls for itself. The loud way works too, at Suspicion +1 whatever the result. The kind in brackets is what one Castle Duty shops for.
  • Try the furniture’s obstacle once that location’s loot has been taken.
  • The way out (Sly or Nimble, or Brawn the loud way) and the lock-up (Sly, or Brawn the loud way; Nimble too to slip free) are always watched.
  • On the map, an eye marks a watched location (one with a watched obstacle besides the furniture’s): the first time anyone arrives, make a Tell check (Chapter 5). A star marks the furniture. Any move takes one Turn (two carrying furniture).
  • The Lantern Night custom and the villagers are colour: the villagers are the faces at watched obstacles.

An Example of Play

Four players raid Thistlewick: Dracula, the Witch, Frankenstein’s Creature and the Werewolf, with their default picks (Butler, Cook, Handyman and Gardener). Suspicion starts at 0; the Limit is 11.

Turn 1. The party splits. The Witch and the Werewolf head for the baker, where the Witch’s Cook Duty gives her an edge; Dracula and the Creature take the china shop, where Dracula’s Butler Duty gives him the same edge. Both count as watched (the china shop for its back gate, though they’ll use the front door), so the Storyteller makes a Tell check at each, a d6 that sets off one arriving Entity’s Tell on 4–6: a 2 and a 1, no Tells. (At the baker, the Witch’s Familiar’s Warning would have needed a second 4–6 too.)

Turn 2. At the baker, the party takes the shuttered window. The Werewolf rolls Nimble d12 with the Mask: 10 + 4 = 14 against 8, a Success. The window is open for both of them now, so the Witch goes on to the shopkeeper (Charm, 8, watched). Her Charm d8 is a d10 here (her Duty’s raise), and she lets the Monster out for the bigger die: 1 + 4 = 5. That is 3 short, Trouble, and the Monster’s 4 beat her trait die’s 1, so it showed. One roll, one rise: Suspicion goes up by the bigger trigger, to 2. The shopkeeper was watching, so the Witch is caught.

She runs. Her Lead starts at 1, and the mob is 8 + half the Suspicion: 9. Each round, the Storyteller rolls the ground on the chase table (Chapter 6). Round 1, a dead end: Wits d12 and the Monster, 12 + 3 = 15, Lead 2. Round 2, another dead end: 2 + 9 = 11, Lead 3, but the Monster showed: Suspicion 4, and the mob is now 10. Round 3, the festival parade, and out comes a sprig of rowan: her Weakness makes her Sly d10 a d8. She spends a charge on her Hedge Spell to raise it back and rolls 5 + 7 = 12: Lead 4, and she escapes. The Monster showed again: Suspicion 6. She is back at the baker, her Turn used up.

At the china shop, Dracula picks the lock of the front door with the Mask: Sly d6, a d8 with his Butler Duty, 7 + 2 = 9 against 8. The Creature takes the back room with Wits d10 and the Mask: 5 + 2 = 7, one short, a Cost. He has the tea service; the Storyteller picks the Cost. Dropping an item would cost nothing (he carries only what this roll won); of the rest, Suspicion +1 hurts most: a teacup smashes. Suspicion 7.

Turn 3. The Witch tries the shopkeeper again, and the Werewolf spends a charge on Good Dog for her (any ability can go on a friend’s roll in the same place, for the charge but not the helper’s action): she rolls the Monster without risking Suspicion. 7 + 4 = 11, a Success, and the wedding cake is hers. Dracula and the Creature move on to the ironmonger, which is watched: the Tell check is a 4, and a d6 between the two arrivals picks the Creature, who stands a head above the crowd. Suspicion 8.

Three Turns gone, nine to go. Both essentials are in hand, but Suspicion is 8 of 11: from here on, every Monster counts.
