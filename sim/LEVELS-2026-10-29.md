# Difficulty levels after the first real game (2026-10-29)

**Richard's decision (2026-10-29):** Easy stays as it is (the players hear the shopping list, then choose monsters and may switch Castle Duties). **Standard:** nobody switches Castle Duties; each monster keeps its own. **Hard:** the players choose their monsters first, then hear the list; Duties locked too. Each level keeps its numbers (Limits 9 / 10 / 13 and the rest).

**What the simulator says:**
- **All three levels stay on target with no number changes:** Easy 91.3% won / 1.0% forked, Standard 74.2% / 3.5%, Hard 55.9% / 9.5% (9,000 raids per level).
- **The new rules barely move the odds** (under 1 point against today's rules as people play them). A Castle Duty only helps on your own rolls at the one or two places with its kind of item, and it's the roll's one raise, so a power often gives the same raise anyway. The levels will *feel* different (less planning around the list on Standard, a blind pick on Hard) more than they play harder.
- **If the goal is a clearly harder game,** the numbers are the lever (the Limit, the way out, the mob). Hard's Limit 14 instead of 13 would add about 4 points of wins; not needed, since Hard is already in its band.
- **Choosing monsters with or without the list** changes Standard by under 1 point either way.

How it was measured: the simulator's standard play gives each monster a Duty at random and picks parties at random. Two new switches (sim/params.mjs TUNING: `dutyPolicy` random / default / match, `partyPick` random / list) model players who hear the list and set their Duties to it, and players who keep each monster's own Duty. The defaults are unchanged, so every earlier report still reads the same.

Targets: won 87–93% / 72–78% / 55–60%; forked ≤2 / 3–7 / 8–12% (Easy / Standard / Hard). ✓ = both in target.
