**What Richard should take from it:**
- **Five players clear a five-item list fast.** One Entity per location, and every item was in hand by Turn 4 on both nights. The 12 Turns never mattered. Only Suspicion did: raid 1 reached 9 of 11, mostly from bad dice where nobody was watching (X1).
- **Rolling the town took longer than playing the raid.** It took 62 rolls and 13 lookups before anyone played (X7).

## 2. What each player experienced

**1. Newcomer: Frankenstein's Creature** (all defaults: Mountain Stride, Strong Back, Handyman)
- *Best:* raid 2, Turn 4. On the Planner's advice he spent his first charge in two nights on Mountain Stride and got through on a Cost: "I GOT THEM."
- *Worst:* five Troubles in a row at the draper's trapdoor (raid 1 Turns 2–4, raid 2 Turns 2–3) with Brawn d12 and the Mask: "I have the biggest die at the table and I keep rolling ones." Each one raised Suspicion, and he felt the 9 of 11 was his fault (3 of those 9 points were).
- *Downtime:* 1 Turn in raid 1, 2 in raid 2 (waiting at the way out).
- *Understood?* He could roll from At the Table without help. He asked what Handyman does (nothing: no tools on the list) and didn't know his Gift could help at the trapdoor until told (F13). He never considered the Monster die. He followed the majority on every vote.
- *Felt like the monster?* No. The Creature is the strong one, and his strength kept failing. His Tell (a head above the crowd) never went off, and Strong Back never mattered because the piece was Bulky.

**2. Daredevil: a Ghost** (Fade, Spectral, Butler)
- *Best:* drifting past the children and away with the armchair, twice, without a roll. Also raid 1 Turn 2, taking the loud way through the geese ("run for it, honking") for the flour.
- *Worst:* raid 2, held back for three Turns so the chair would cost one Suspicion ("Three Turns of you lot standing around so I can do one thing?"), and outvoted on rolling the way out. He never had a close Monster roll or a reason to overdraw.
- *Downtime:* none on either night. He was the busiest player at the table.
- *Understood?* Yes, once the Planner pointed out at the picks what Spectral does with a group obstacle.
- *Felt like the monster?* Yes: a cold spot on the bakery roof, a chair floating down the high street in fog. His Weakness (Cold Iron, Always) never came up, because there was no chase.

**3. Planner: Jekyll & Hyde** (Doctor's Bag, Practised Hand, Librarian)
- *Best:* raid 1 Turn 4. Hyde got a Critical on the trapdoor after the Creature's third failure, then his armchair timing landed exactly on 1 Suspicion.
- *Worst:* his Librarian edge did nothing (his only bookseller roll was Jekyll's Charm, already a d12), and Doctor's Bag never had a roll to raise. Most of his night went on reading rules for the others: he did nearly all of the in-play lookups.
- *Downtime:* 1 Turn in raid 1, 3 in raid 2.
- *Understood?* Yes, best at the table.
- *Felt like the monster?* Partly. The doctor fumbling at the counter and then Hyde lifting the trapdoor worked as a story. But the Monster never showed on a Jekyll roll, so Hyde never took over on his own, and the Draught was only a die swap. His Tell went off once.

**4. Hoarder: a Witch** (A Potion for That, Fly by Night, Cook)
- *Best:* raid 1 Turn 4. A Potion for That (Wits d12) plus the Ghost's Fade got her over the butcher's gate for the essential honey.
- *Worst:* raid 1 Turn 3, standing at that gate too wary to roll ("the mob would be 11"). Raid 2, three Turns of waiting at the way out.
- *Charges:* she spent 1 and then 0, and took 2 and then 3 home unspent, saved "for an emergency" that never came.
- *Downtime:* 2 Turns in raid 1, 4 in raid 2.
- *Understood?* Yes.
- *Felt like the monster?* Weakly. Her signature Hedge Spell was never used: the only rolls that needed help were already d12s, which can't be raised. Her Tell never went off, and Fly by Night needs a chase. The potion was her one witchy moment.

**5. Roleplayer: the Invisible Man** (Poltergeist, Out of Sight, Tailor)
- *Best:* raid 1 Turn 3, a Critical on the tavern wall: "Gentlemen, the cheese has left the building. Unaccompanied." He also rolled the way out for everyone on both nights, and spent raid 2 carrying a sack of flour (his own Weakness).
- *Worst:* outvoted on going to the draper, and then Hyde, not he, got "his" bandages. Trouble on the wall in raid 1, Turn 2.
- *Downtime:* 1 Turn in raid 1, 2 in raid 2 (filled by voicing the Invisible Man chatting up the bell-ringers at the gate).
- *Understood?* Yes.
- *Felt like the monster?* More than anyone. Unseen was on every roll that mattered, and the flour joke wrote itself. Poltergeist was never used, and his Tailor edge gave nothing (others got the bandages).

- **The build is the long wait.** Five players sit with nothing to do for 35–88 minutes while the Storyteller rolls and writes out 12 obstacles. That is longer than either raid's play (37 and 26 minutes by the formula). If the Storyteller builds the town before the session, the session drops to about 1 h – 1 h 15, under the book's "2–3 hours".
- **Raid 1, Turn 4:** about 5 minutes while the Planner worked out the armchair timing (two lookups) and the others waited.
- **Raid 2, Turns 5–6:** four players with nothing to do while the Ghost went for the chair. Only a couple of real minutes, but apart from the Roleplayer chatting to the bell-ringers, nobody had anything to do.
- **A five-player Turn is long.** Raid 1's Turn 2 (five rolls, a Cost choice, two lookups) took roughly 10 minutes, so each player waited about 8 minutes between their own rolls. That's fine at this pace, but it would grow fast with a chase in the middle of a Turn.
- No player was ever stuck in a chase or the lock-up while the others played on, because neither happened.

## 5. Tweak candidates

Numbers below are either exact dice arithmetic (marked) or what happened at this table. Nothing here is a measured win rate; each says what I'd want simulated.

**X1. Five players make a Standard town easy.**
- *What happened:* each of the five took one location. Every item was in hand by Turn 4 on both nights, both ended in a Grand Year (Turns 6 and 7), and 10 of 15 charges went home unspent both times. Turns were never a constraint, and Suspicion was the only pressure. Chapter 4's "there's rarely time to visit every place together" doesn't apply at five.
- *How often:* I'd expect it in most five-player Standard raids with a five-item list.
- *Options:* (a) scale the list with the party, one more extra for each player over four; (b) scale Suspicion with the party (a Limit one lower, or Tell checks per Entity arriving); (c) leave it, and tell five-player tables to pick Hard.
- *Recommendation:* simulate first. Win, Grand Year and "Turn the list is complete" by party size (3, 4, 5) on rolled Standard towns. If five is clearly easier than four, (a) changes the least.

**X2. Furniture is cheap with the right timing, and free of rolls with Spectral.**
- *What happened:* the chair cost exactly 1 Suspicion each night (take it as the carrier sets off, roll the way out as soon as he arrives), and no roll at all, because the rolled furniture obstacle was a group obstacle that Spectral walks past. The Daredevil's push for furniture never carried any risk.
- *How often:* the timing works for any party, every time. The Spectral case needs a group result on the furniture's obstacle (5 of 20 on the obstacle table: exact) and a Ghost with its default Perk (5 of 8 five-player parties include the Ghost: exact). That's roughly one five-player rolled town in six.
- *Options:* (a) Spectral doesn't work on a furniture obstacle; (b) Chapter 8: reroll a group result for the furniture's obstacle; (c) make the furniture cost more up front (say, +1 when taken as well as each Turn), so timing can't shrink it to 1.
- *Recommendation:* (b), a one-line build rule that leaves the Ghost's Perk alone everywhere else. Simulate (c) only if Grand Year rates look high generally.

**X3. Two of five Castle Duties did nothing.**
- *What happened:* the Ghost's Butler and the Creature's Handyman had nothing on the list, and the Librarian edge was wasted on a d12.
- *How often:* by the shopping table's odds, a given kind is missing from a five-item list (5/6)^5 ≈ 40% of the time (exact). A five-player party therefore averages about two dead Duties.
- *Options:* (a) roll the list before players pick Duties (sequencing advice, no rule change; it can't help five players when the list has three kinds); (b) a fallback edge for a Duty with nothing on the list (a new rule); (c) leave it: the Duty is flavour plus a small edge.
- *Recommendation:* (a) now. (b) only if Richard wants every player to get a moment from it.

**X4. Waiting at the way out.**
- *What happened:* in raid 2, three or four players waited two or three Turns at the way out while the Ghost went for the chair, which is exactly what the cheapest timing rewards. Downtime came to 5 player-Turns in raid 1 and 11 in raid 2 (§2).
- *How often:* whenever the furniture is fetched last.
- *Options:* (a) leave it: waiting Turns take seconds; (b) give Entities at the way out something to do (new content); (c) advice in Chapter 8's Pacing: whoever is waiting voices the villagers (the book already says this for the lock-up).
- *Recommendation:* (c).

**X5. Early Costs become Suspicion.**
- *What happened:* 2 of 2 Costs became Suspicion +1, because Chapter 8's examples only say when the other three Costs bite, and that's late in the night.
- *How often:* most Costs before about Turn 8.
- *Options:* (a) advice: "early in the night, prefer a lost Turn or a smaller die"; (b) keep: Suspicion is the game's clock; (c) the Storyteller offers two Costs and the player picks.
- *Recommendation:* (a). Simulate the win-rate effect of "no Suspicion Costs before Turn 6" against the current choice.

**X6. The Newcomer's Mask at Difficulty 10.**
- *What happened:* Brawn d12 + Mask against 10 is 54% Success, 17% Cost, 29% Trouble per try (exact). He took Trouble five times in a row (about a 1-in-500 streak: bad luck), all at a place nobody watched, so every one raised Suspicion. He got through only with his Gift: Nimble d8 against 8, 56/23/21 (exact), which is safer than his d12 against 10. He never touched the Monster die.
- *How often:* the streak was luck. But a Mask-only new player meets Difficulty 10 on about 3 obstacles in 10, and falls short on about 46% of those even with a d12 (a Cost or Trouble; Trouble alone 29%, exact).
- *Options:* (a) an At the Table line: "Against 10 or more, the Mask often falls short: think about the Monster or an ability"; (b) Chapter 8 advice: give a new player a Difficulty 8 job first; (c) leave it.
- *Recommendation:* (a) and (b). Both are advice; neither changes a number.

**X7. Rolling the town takes longer than the raid.**
- *What happened:* 62 rolls and 13 lookups: 88 minutes by the formula, about 35–45 in practice, against 37 and 26 minutes of play.
- *How often:* every rolled town.
- *Options:* (a) Chapter 8: "Roll the town before the session (about half an hour)"; (b) a printable town sheet with the rows to fill in; (c) a quicker procedure, such as one Difficulty and one watched roll per location instead of per obstacle (a rule change).
- *Recommendation:* (a) and (b). Consider (c) only if Richard wants towns rolled at the table.

**X8. Charges hardly mattered at five.**
- *What happened:* 10 of 15 unspent each night. The Hoarder took 2 and then 3 home, and the Newcomer 3 and then 2. Two Criticals refunded charges in raid 1.
- *How often:* with X1.
- *Options:* (a) fix X1 first and look again; (b) fewer charges for big parties (a rule change); (c) leave it.
- *Recommendation:* (a). Don't change charges on their own.

## 6. The story

*As the table would tell it the next week:*

The bells went every hour that night, which the Witch said was a good sign: if they're still ringing them, they don't believe in us. We split five ways. The Ghost drifted over the bakery roof, gave the mayor a cold spot in the middle of his peal, then ran honking through the goose yard and came out with a sack of flour. The doctor tried to buy black-edged paper with the wrong hand showing, and a gang of children with handbells saw every hair on it. At the draper's, the Creature heaved at a cellar trapdoor three times and three times it didn't move, until the doctor came round, had a quick drink, and Hyde lifted it on the first go. At the butcher's, the Witch stood at the back gate and wouldn't touch it until the Ghost turned up with his fog. Then she mixed something in a jar and the bolt slid back by itself: one jar of honey. The Invisible Man fell off the tavern wall, then walked out with a whole wheel of cheese floating at waist height.

By the fourth bell we had everything, and Suspicion was at eight of eleven. The Ghost said the bookshop had a wingback armchair "just sitting there", and the Witch said absolutely not, and we voted her down four to one. The children crowding the bookshop never saw the Ghost go past them. All they saw was an armchair gliding down the high street in a cold mist, which is when the bells stopped. The Invisible Man talked us out through the town gate in a voice from nowhere, and we were home by the sixth bell. Grand Year: the chair's in the great hall, and we all pretend it was always there.

The next Lantern Night we went back to the same town. The Creature finally beat his trapdoor (well, he went round it), the Invisible Man carried a sack of flour all night without sneezing, and the Ghost brought home a second armchair that nobody needed.

**Villagers:** Chapter 8 says to roll one "whenever a watched obstacle needs a face, or a Cost needs someone to cause it", so the Storyteller rolls them in play (who d6, doing d6), not in the build. Each roll is logged where it happens.

**Map:** Chapter 8 has no map table or map step for a rolled town. The table used Chapter 4's default, "any move takes one Turn unless the map says otherwise", for every move (way out, five locations, lock-up), and Chapter 8's "In a rolled town no entrance is small". Logged as F2.

Things the table noticed while building (all logged in §4): the random order puts *the shopkeeper behind the counter* as the butcher's way in with *a bolted back gate* behind it, and *a locked strongbox* as the tavern's way in; a doorman at the draper "checking invitations" that nobody watches; the furniture's obstacle is a *group* obstacle; and the bookseller counts as watched even if the party takes its unwatched tug-of-war.

**Build time.** 62 rolls (R1–R62) and 13 lookups (the Rolling a Town steps, the difficulty table, the shopping table, the essentials rule, the custom table, the places, the obstacle-count row, the obstacle table, the Difficulty row, the watched row, the second-way-in rule, the ceiling, the furniture table and its location rule). By the session formula that is 62 + 26 = **88 minutes**. That formula overstates table rolls in a build: a d20 read off a table takes well under a minute. A realistic estimate with one Storyteller rolling and writing a town sheet is **35–45 minutes** (about 30 seconds a roll plus writing 12 obstacles out by hand), and the players have nothing to do during it. See X7.

## 8. Raid 1: the play, Turn by Turn

Suspicion 0, Limit 11. Twelve Turns. The party starts at the way out.

**The plan (party decision 1).** The Planner proposed one Entity per location: the Creature to the draper (unwatched, Brawn d12 at the trapdoor: "a safe first job"), the Witch to the butcher (Sly d12 there with her Cook edge), the Ghost to the baker (Spectral walks past the rooftops), Jekyll to the bookseller (Charm d12 at the shopkeeper), the Invisible Man to the tavern cellar (unwatched, the garden wall).
*Argument.* Roleplayer: "The bandages are literally mine, I should get them." Planner: "Your best there is Charm d8 against 10. The Creature has Brawn d12, and nobody watches the draper, so the new player can't get caught." Hoarder: "The butcher is watched three times over. Why me?" Planner: "Because your Sly is a d12 there and the honey is essential." Vote: Planner, Daredevil, Newcomer for; Hoarder and Roleplayer against. **3–2, the plan stands.** Roleplayer: "Fine. But I'm wearing them home."

### Turn 1 (moves)
Everyone moves to their location. The Ghost passes the baker's rooftops (group) with Spectral, at no action, in the Turn he moves.
Tell checks, first arrival at each watched location: butcher R69 = 3, nothing. Baker R70 = 5, **a Tell**: only the Ghost arrived, so it's his Cold Spot. Villager (R72–73): *the mayor, in his best costume, practising the bells*; his breath fogs over the handbell. Bookseller R71 = 6, **a Tell**: Jekyll's Wrong Hand. Villager (R74–75): *a gang of children, practising the bells*, who stare at the doctor's hairy hand. (The obstacle is "the shopkeeper behind the counter", so the Storyteller made the children witnesses at the counter: F5.)
**Suspicion 2.**

### Turn 2 (first rolls)
- **Creature** (draper, trapdoor, Brawn 10, not watched): Brawn d12 + Mask, 5 + 1 = 6, **Trouble**. Suspicion 3, no chase (nobody watching). Newcomer: "Is that bad?"
- **Witch** (butcher, geese, Sly 8, watched): Sly d12 (Cook) + Mask, 7 + 1 = 8, **Success**. The geese are beaten for the party.
- **Ghost** (baker, geese, watched): takes the loud way, Nimble ("run for it, honking"), d12 + Mask, 10 + 6 = 16, **Success**, loud: Suspicion 4. The geese were the baker's last obstacle: **the sack of flour** is in his hand. Daredevil: "Flour! Keep it away from the invisible guy." Planner: "You had Sly d10 against 8 for free."
- **Jekyll** (bookseller, shopkeeper, Charm 8, watched): Charm d12 + Mask, 2 + 5 = 7, one short, **a Cost**; he has **the writing paper**. The Storyteller's options: drop an item is out (he carries only what this roll won), lose a Turn and a smaller die don't bite yet on Turn 2, so **Suspicion +1**: the children see a hairy hand vanish under the counter. Suspicion 5. (Logged as X5: early in the night, Suspicion is almost always the Cost that "hurts most".)
- **Invisible Man** (tavern cellar, garden wall, Nimble 8, not watched): Nimble d8 + Mask, 1 + 3 = 4, **Trouble**. Suspicion 6.

**Suspicion 6** after two Turns, five points of it from bad dice at places nobody watched. Hoarder: "Half way to the Limit and we've got two things."

### Turn 3
- **Creature**: trapdoor again, Mask, 2 + 1 = 3, **Trouble**. Suspicion 7. Newcomer: "I have the biggest die at the table and I keep rolling ones."
- **Invisible Man**: spends a charge on Unseen and rolls Nimble d8 + Monster d10: 7 + 7 = 14, **Success, and a Critical** (doubles): the charge comes back. **The cheese** is his. Roleplayer, as the Invisible Man: "Gentlemen, the cheese has left the building. Unaccompanied."
- **Witch** waits at the bolted back gate (Brawn 10, watched). Hoarder: "The mob would be 11 if I'm caught. I'm not rolling that alone." (Mob = 8 + half of 7.)
- **Ghost** moves to the butcher to lend his Fade to the Witch's roll next Turn (party decision 2: the Planner's call, nobody objected).
- **Jekyll**, seeing the Creature fail again, moves to the draper: "Two shots at that trapdoor next Turn."

**Suspicion 7.**

### Turn 4
- **Creature**: trapdoor, Mask, 3 + 2 = 5, **Trouble** (third in a row). Suspicion 8.
- **Witch** spends a charge on A Potion for That (Wits d12 instead of Brawn) and the Ghost spends one on Fade for her roll (the Monster without risking Suspicion; helping costs his charge, not his action). Wits d12 + Monster d10: 9 + 4 = 13, **Success**. **The honey** is hers. Hoarder: "That's one charge. I'm keeping the other two."

Newcomer: "Whatever you think." **4–1 for the chair.**

**Suspicion 8.**

**Out of town on Turn 6, Suspicion 9 of 11, no chases.**
