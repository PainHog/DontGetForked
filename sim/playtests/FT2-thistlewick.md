# FT2: a full table in Thistlewick (Standard), five players and a Storyteller

*Agent playtest, 2026-10-07. A simulated first session for a full table: five players with different habits and a Storyteller. Rules source: only the rulebook text, `book/src/chapters/*.html` read as text, including the Thistlewick map's title and labels. The simulator, `docs/` and the Foundry code were not used as rules. Every random result is a real `node -e` roll (Math.random), numbered R1 onward and listed in the appendix. Where the book is silent or unclear I made a ruling, noted it in the log, and listed it under Friction.*

---

## 1. Summary

The table played **two raids** on Thistlewick. The first ended on Turn 6, after about an hour and ten minutes with setup, so the same five played a second night (the brief asks for one only if the first ends *before* Turn 6, but six Turns with no final flight left too much of the game unplayed). The full logs are at the end (Logs A–C).

| | Night 1 | Night 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Plan | Five-way split: each Entity to its own Duty’s shop | Pairs: Witch + Werewolf, Dracula + Ghost; the Creature alone |
| **Result** | **Grand Year** | **Partial** |
| Turns used | 6 of 12 | 6 of 12 |
| Suspicion at the end | 7 / 11 | 8 / 11 |
| Items home | 4 of 5 (the top hat lost) + the gilt mirror | 4 of 5 (the wedding cake, an essential, lost) |

| Left behind | none | none |

What stood out:
- **Five players run out of town long before dawn.** Both nights ended on Turn 6 of 12, in about 45 minutes of play each (plus 20 minutes of setup the first time), without reaching dawn, the Limit or the final flight. Turns never put the party under pressure; only Suspicion did. Even pairs (night 2), the slow plan, finished by Turn 6.
- **Every Duty matched.** The Duty clash moved the Ghost to Tailor, and then the five Duties covered all five list items, so every location had someone with a raise.
- **Local chases were over in one roll.** Both started at Lead 1, and both ended on their first round with a Trouble and a capture.
- **One capture decided night 2 on Turn 2.** The Newcomer’s single chase roll lost the wedding cake for the night. From then on the best possible result was a Partial, and the furniture was worthless.
- **Charges went mostly unspent.** The median player spent 1 of 3 each night. Only the Daredevil emptied his.
- **Friction:** no blockers, 1 major, 8 minor and 5 wording (§4).

## 2. What each player experienced

“Downtime” counts the Turns in which the player only moved, waited or lost the Turn: 12 Turns over the two nights. “Rolls” counts the player’s own trait rolls (not Tell checks).

**Newcomer: a Witch.** Downtime 4 + 3 = **7 of 12**. Rolls 5. Charges 0, then 2.
- *Best:* the wedding cake on her second roll ever (night 1, Turn 3), in character (“Is it spoken for?”). In night 2, spending Hedge Spell from inside her cell to help the Ghost’s rescue roll: “I helped!”
- *Worst:* night 2, Turn 2. Caught at the shopkeeper, captured on the first round of the chase, and the cake gone for the night: “Sorry. Sorry, everyone.” Earlier, her first roll of the game was a Critical that paid nothing because she hadn’t spent a charge (“Did I do something good?”).
- *Understood?* Her own rolls, yes, once the Storyteller showed her which way in used her best die. Charges, no: she never spent one unprompted, and in the chase she didn’t think to raise her own die (At the Table’s “abilities on your own roll only” didn’t prompt her). At the Table never told her that being captured would cost her the cake.
- *Felt like a Witch?* Partly. The cat (Familiar’s Warning) was her witchiest moment, twice. Her default Gift, Broomstick, opens an approach with her d4 Nimble, so it was never worth using. On her own rolls her Duty raise already used the roll’s one raise, so her signature (Hedge Spell) did nothing until she was in the lock-up.

**Daredevil: the Werewolf.** Downtime 3 + 3 = **6 of 12**. Rolls 6. Charges 3, then 3 (the only player who emptied them).
- *Best:* night 1, Turn 5. The guard dog beaten the loud way while the Monster showed under Good Dog: “a very large dog chasing a guard dog round the yard”. Through the Hedge on the night watchman twice (Brawn d12 against 8, a sure thing).
- *Worst:* night 2, Turn 3. The cake was gone, and the Planner told him the mirror no longer counted: “So I can’t even go for the mirror?” He took the loud way at the geese both nights (+2 Suspicion for nothing, the second time at 6 of 11, to the table’s groans).
- *Understood?* Yes. He never got chased, which was what he took Night Runner for.
- *Felt like the Werewolf?* Yes. Good Dog and his Tell (eyebrows that meet, which went off twice) were the table’s running jokes.

**Planner: Dracula.** Downtime 3 + 5 = **8 of 12**. Rolls **3**. Charges 2, then 1.
- *Best:* night 1, Turns 4–6. He sent the Witch first to the way out so her cat covered the Tell check, and planned the mirror run so it was taken and carried out in time for one Suspicion. Carrying a mirror he can’t see himself in.
- *Worst:* night 2. A lost Turn (the Cost) on Turn 3, then waiting on Turn 4. He rolled **once** all night.
- *Understood?* Best of the table. He did about two-thirds of the rule lookups and was never bored: he planned everyone’s moves, even in the Turns he waited.
- *Felt like Dracula?* Not much. In two nights he never rolled his Charm d12, and Mesmerise and Hypnotic Eyes never came up: Bat made him a burglar who turns into a bat. His most Dracula moment, the mirror, was the Roleplayer’s idea.

**Hoarder: Frankenstein’s Creature.** Downtime 4 + 2 = **6 of 12**. Rolls 5. Charges 0, then 1.
- *Best:* night 2, Turn 6. At Suspicion 8 he finally spent Hovel Watcher on the way-out roll, “because this is the emergency”, and it got everyone home.
- *Worst:* night 2, Turn 5. He refused Brute Force at the (unwatched) nosy neighbour and rolled his Sly d6. The Cost took Suspicion to 8. In night 1 he turned down the Planner twice and went home with all three charges.
- *Understood?* Yes. He was alone at an easy shop both nights.
- *Felt like the Creature?* A little: he lifted the cart with Brawn d12. His Tell never went off and he never let the Monster out, so nothing monstrous happened to him. Flat, by his own choice.

**Roleplayer: a Ghost.** Downtime 2 + 3 = **5 of 12**. Rolls **8** (the most). Charges 1, then 0.
- *Best:* walking out of the lock-up on a natural 12 (night 1). The rescue in night 2, chains rattling so loudly the jailer laughed (Rattle). Rolling the way out both nights.
- *Worst:* night 1, Turn 3. He talked to the guard dog instead of taking the Planner’s cleverer line, was cornered in a dead end on the first round, and lost the top hat, his own Duty’s item. The epilogue’s “string and hope” line was “on me”. In night 2 his own Tell at the lock-up cost a point.
- *Understood?* Yes.
- *Felt like a Ghost?* In voice, yes (Whisper, Rattle, the cold spot). Not in the one moment that mattered: he asked to walk through the cell wall, and couldn’t, because the lock-up lists Sly, so Through the Wall can’t open an approach there.

## 3. Table time

Counting about 1 minute per roll, 1 minute per party decision and 2 minutes per rules lookup, plus 20 minutes for choosing Entities:

| | Rolls | Decisions | Lookups | Minutes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Choosing Entities and picks (R1) | — | — | — | 20 |
| Night 1 (R2–R21) | 20 | 7 | 11 | 20 + 7 + 22 = **49** |
| Night 2 (R22–R41) | 20 | 9 | 7 | 20 + 9 + 14 = **43** |
| **Total** | 40 | 16 | 18 | **about 112 minutes (just under 2 hours)** |

So the first raid, setup included, took about **69 minutes**, and the second about 43. Chapter 1 says “A raid runs 2–3 hours.”

- **Decisions counted.** Night 1: the split (D1), the Ghost’s hat-or-dog debate, the loud geese, the Hoarder’s strongbox charge, the furniture vote (D2), the Witch-first arrival, and the way-out roller with Hovel Watcher (D3). Night 2: pairs (D5), Good Dog for the Witch, the regroup after the capture with the mirror dropped (D6), coaching the Witch to spend, the rescue with Hedge Spell through the bars, the loud geese again, Brute Force refused, the group arrival at the way out, and the way-out roll (D7).
- **Lookups counted.** Night 1: whether the hatter is watched; the Critical refund; which Costs are allowed and the “drop an item” rule; local chase numbers and an Always Weakness; Whisper in a chase; capture, the loot taken back and whether the mirror was still on (ruling R-A); slipping free; Through the Wall at the lock-up; Familiar’s Warning at the way out; taking and carrying the piece; leaving, and a Cost at the way out. Night 2: Costs again (lose a Turn); capture of an essential, re-stealing it and the result cap; what counts as half the list; a captive’s abilities (ruling R-B); rescue and the lock-up’s Tell check; arriving together (ruling R-C); furniture without a Win.
- **Long waits:**
  - Night 1, Turn 3: the Ghost’s dog roll, chase, capture and three lookups took about 10 minutes, while the other four, finished with their shops, watched.
  - Night 1, Turns 4–6: the Witch and the Creature sat at the way out for three Turns (about 15 minutes) with nothing to do.
  - Night 2, Turns 2–3: the Witch’s capture, the argument over the lost cake and the replan took about 10 minutes. Only the Witch and the Planner were really involved.
  - Night 2, Turns 3–6: Dracula lost a Turn and then waited. The Planner rolled once all night and was kept busy only by planning.
- **Turn length at five.** A Turn with five actions in three to five places ran 5–15 minutes. A player who only moved waited through four other actions. Talking over a plan across the table (“use Hovel Watcher!”) filled most of that time, and the waiting was fine while something was at stake.

## 5. Tweak candidates

Each is my judgement from two nights. Nothing here is a measured number; each says what I’d want simulated first.

**T1. Raids are short at five players.**
- *What happened:* both nights ended on Turn 6 of 12, in about 45 minutes of play each. Nobody saw dawn, the Limit or the final flight, the game’s big set piece. Chapter 4’s advice (“The party may split up, and on Standard and Hard it usually should: there’s rarely time to visit every place together”) isn’t true at five: the five-way split had every item it would get in hand by Turn 3 (Turn 5 for the mirror), and pairs had what they could get by Turn 5.
- *How often:* most five-player raids on Easy and Standard, I’d expect, and many four-player ones.
- *Options:*
  - (a) Keep the rules. Change Chapter 1 to “a raid runs 1–2 hours” and suggest two raids an evening.
  - (b) Scale the night by party size (fewer Turns for five).
  - (c) Give five-player parties more to do (a sixth list item, or one more watched obstacle in a premade town).
- *Recommendation:* (a) now. Simulate (b) and (c): Turns used, win rate and final-flight rate by party size (3, 4, 5) on Standard.

**T2. Five Duties meet five list items.**
- *What happened:* the Duty rule moved the Ghost onto Tailor, and every location then had an Entity with the raise. In night 1 the ten rolls made at a Duty location gave 8 Successes, 1 Cost and 1 Trouble (the guard dog, at 10).
- *How often:* with five of the six Duties in play and five items, I’d expect many towns to give four or more matches (worth simulating).
- *Options:*
  - (a) Keep it: it feels good and teaches the Duties.
  - (b) At five players, the Duty raise applies only at the location’s first obstacle.
  - (c) The Duty raise only at essentials.
- *Recommendation:* (a), unless the simulator shows five-player Standard well above the win-rate target. Simulate with and without Duty matches at five.

**T3. Local chases end in one roll.**
- *What happened:* both chases started at Lead 1, and a single Trouble on round 1 meant capture. The chase, a highlight, lasted one ground roll and one trait roll each time.
- *How often:* every local chase carries that first-roll risk. From the odds, roughly one in six to one in three per round depending on the die (to be simulated, not taken as fact).
- *Options:*
  - (a) Keep it: it’s a sharp threat.
  - (b) Start a local chase at Lead 2 and escape at 5: the same distance, but one Trouble no longer captures. Night Runner (Werewolf) would then need a new edge.
  - (c) A Trouble on the first round of a local chase can’t drop the Lead below 1.
- *Recommendation:* simulate (b) against (a) for capture rate, rounds per chase and the Suspicion chases add. Then choose; (b) is simpler to teach than (c).

**T4. Capturing an essential decides the night early.**
- *What happened:* night 2, Turn 2. One chase roll by the Newcomer took the wedding cake for the night. From then on the best result was a Partial, the furniture was worthless (a Grand Year needs a Win), and the Daredevil lost his goal. The rest of the night was played out knowing the result.
- *How often:* whenever whoever carries an essential is captured. I’d guess one raid in three or four, given how often essentials are taken early.
- *Options:*
  - (a) Keep it: capture should hurt.
  - (b) What a captive carried waits at the lock-up and comes back with a rescue or a slip-free.
  - (c) A taken-back essential returns to its location, which can be raided again.
- *Recommendation:* (b). Capture stays costly, but the rest of the night becomes a rescue story instead of a foregone Partial. Simulate the win rate and Turns used under (b).

**T5. Charges go unspent.**
- *What happened:* 6 and 7 of 15 charges were spent. The median player spent 1 of 3 each night, below the “at least half” bar in the lessons from the first game. The Newcomer and the Hoarder spent nothing in night 1, and the Planner and the Ghost ended night 2 with 2 and 3.
- *How often:* every table with cautious or new players.
- *Options:*
  - (a) Keep it (Chapter 2 already says “spend them”).
  - (b) Put a one-line nudge where players look: the Entity Sheet and At the Table (“Unspent charges are lost at dawn”).
  - (c) A small reward for spending, designed by the author.
- *Recommendation:* (b), a wording-only change. Check charge spend per player in the simulator with a cautious policy.

**T6. Thin spotlight at five.**
- *What happened:* own rolls per player over two raids ranged from 3 (the Planner) to 8 (the Ghost). Dracula never rolled his Charm d12 or used Mesmerise. Thistlewick’s Charm obstacles went to the Cook and to the loud Werewolf.
- *How often:* likely at any five-player table, since five players share about 15–20 rolls a raid.
- *Options:*
  - (a) Accept it.
  - (b) Storyteller advice in Chapter 8 for five players: point each player at one obstacle that suits their best trait or signature.
  - (c) Premade towns with one obstacle per signature trait.
- *Recommendation:* (b).

**T7. The Newcomer’s default kit.**
- *What happened:* the Witch’s default Gift, Broomstick, opens an approach with her d4 Nimble, and she never used it. At her own Duty location, her signature (a raise) adds nothing, because the Duty already used the one raise.
- *How often:* every new player given the Witch. On paper, none of the other seven defaults uses a d4.
- *Options:*
  - (a) Keep it.
  - (b) Make another Witch Gift the default (A Potion for That uses her Wits d12).
  - (c) A sheet note: “Hedge Spell works on your friends’ rolls too.”
- *Recommendation:* audit each Entity’s defaults for “opens with your d4”. Changing the Witch’s default is the author’s call; add (c) in any case.

**T8. A Critical with nothing to refund.**
- *What happened:* the Newcomer’s first roll was a Critical and gave nothing.
- *How often:* most Criticals early in a raid, and those of players who don’t spend.
- *Options:*
  - (a) Keep it, with the wording fix F12.
  - (b) Let it bank a charge above the starting number for that raid.
  - (c) Give an unrefundable Critical a non-Suspicion edge, such as the next roll’s trait die a size larger, still within one raise.
- *Recommendation:* (a). The lessons warn against good results undoing the threat track, and both (b) and (c) need simulating.

**T9. The Ghost and the lock-up.**
- *What happened:* the Ghost could not walk through the cell wall (the lock-up lists Sly), and his Cold Iron Weakness doesn’t apply to the iron bars. Both are correct by the book, but both jar.
- *How often:* every time a Ghost is captured.
- *Options:*
  - (a) Keep it.
  - (b) A flavour line explaining why (author’s words).
  - (c) Let Through the Wall open the lock-up for the Ghost itself.
- *Recommendation:* (b). Low priority.

## 6. The story

*As the table would tell it the next day.*

The first year we split up like a heist film, one monster to a shop. The Witch slipped through the crowded shop on doubles (which, it turns out, do nothing) and charmed the baker out of the wedding cake. Dracula turned into a bat to get into the china shop and sent a teapot lid rolling across the floor on the way out. The Creature just moved the cart. The Werewolf ran honking through a yard of geese for no reason at all, then went straight through the night watchman. And the Ghost, the hatter’s own Tailor, had the top hat in one go, tried to sweet-talk the guard dog for the gilt mirror, failed, got pinned in a dead end by the blacksmith’s boy with a horseshoe, and was in the lock-up before anyone looked up from their dice. He walked out the next Turn as if the bars weren’t there. So the Werewolf went back for the mirror, and half the town watched a very large dog chase a guard dog round the yard. Dracula carried the mirror home, the one man in Thistlewick who can’t see himself in it, and we were out by midnight: a Grand Year, minus one top hat. “Next year’s disguises are held together with string and hope.” The Ghost said sorry.

The second year we went in pairs so nobody would be alone, and it lasted one Turn. The Werewolf opened the baker’s window, the Witch let the monster out to charm the shopkeeper, and it went wrong: she bolted into the back alleys and was caught at the first corner. The cake went back in the window with a sign saying NOT FOR MONSTERS, and we spent the rest of the night knowing it could only be a Partial. The Ghost came for her, rattling his chains so loudly the jailer laughed, while she cast her spell through the bars to help him. The Werewolf ran at the geese again (we were on six; he didn’t care), the Creature took the top hat and got shouted at by the neighbour, and at Suspicion 8 the Hoarder finally spent a charge, on the last roll of the night. Four things home and no cake: turnip soup until spring. The Newcomer volunteered for the draughty tower.

Table talk while picking:
- **Daredevil:** “Through the Hedge. It *goes straight through*. That’s the whole character.” Night Runner, because “I’m planning on being chased.”
- **Planner** read every Perk aloud and kept the defaults: Bat turns Dracula’s bad Sly d6 and terrible Wits d4 into Nimble d10, and most of Thistlewick is Sly and Wits. “I’m the face. Hypnotic Eyes.”
