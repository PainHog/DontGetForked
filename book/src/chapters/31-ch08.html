Chapter 8

Building a Town

Every raid is on a new town. There are three ways to build one, all from the same parts — obstacles, locations, and the way out.

Three Ways

  • A premade raid: a fixed set of locations and obstacles, ready to run. Chapter 9 has three, one for each difficulty, each with its map.
  • A map: a set of locations the party picks its route through.
  • The random tables: roll the locations, obstacles, loot and villagers. A ceiling on the hardest obstacles keeps a rolled town playing at the difficulty it promises.

Lantern Night

On the festival night, everyone dresses up as a monster and carries a lantern to keep the real ones away. Every town keeps it its own way: roll a d6 or pick a custom for each town. Customs are flavour, not rules.

d6Local custom
1Turnip lanterns in every window.
2A costume parade through the square at midnight.
3A masked ball at the mayor’s house.
4Pies left on doorsteps “for the wanderers”.
5A bonfire in the square, and a straw monster burned at dawn.
6Bells rung every hour to keep the real monsters away. They never work.

Rolling a Town

  1. Locations: one for each item on the list, at one of the places its kind is found (the shopping table below lists them): a d3 or pick, one item per place; if a kind has more items than places, two may share a place.
  2. Obstacles: roll a d20 for how many each location has.
  3. Each obstacle (both versions of the first, its two ways in, and the furniture’s extra one, 2 harder): the obstacle table for what it is, a d20 for its Difficulty, a d10 for whether it’s watched. For the second way in, roll the obstacle table again until its quiet way is a different trait.
  4. The ceiling: at most one Difficulty 12 in an Easy or Standard town, two in a Hard town (the furniture’s obstacle counts by its roll, before its +2). Reroll any extra.

Obstacles

Give each obstacle one or two traits that work. When there are two, usually one is the loud way. Mark which obstacles are watched — Trouble there gets an Entity caught — and which are group obstacles that each Entity must roll for itself. Give each location two versions of its first obstacle, with different traits: its two ways in.

Running the Villagers and the Mob

Villagers and witnesses. Everyone in town is in costume and in a good mood, and they see what they expect to see. A watched obstacle means someone is looking: roll a villager to give them a face. Play the town busy and cheerful, until it isn’t.

Choosing Costs. Pick the one that hurts most right now, but never one that costs nothing (Chapter 3). A lost Turn bites near dawn, a smaller die before a hard roll, “drop an item” when someone carries loot, Suspicion +1 near the Limit. Always say what happened: the baker’s wife looks twice.

Suspicion. Keep the track where everyone can see it, and let the town’s mood follow it: whispers, then pointing, then torches.

The mob. Once the hunt is on, the mob is like the weather: always there, always closer. Read the ground off the chase table every round. When a Weakness comes into play (Chapter 6), make it a moment.

The lock-up. Capture is a setback, not an ending. The captive can try to slip free every Turn, and the others can come for them (Chapter 6). Keep the captive’s player busy: let them voice a jailer or a villager.

Pacing. Count the Turns aloud, and warn when three are left. A raid should fit in two to three hours.
