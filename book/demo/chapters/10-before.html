For the Storyteller

Before You Play

This is a complete evening of Don’t Get Forked for 3–5 players who have never played. It teaches the game as it goes, so nobody needs to read the rulebook first, not even you. Read this page and the next before your guests arrive; everything after that you can read aloud at the table.

From the Designer

This is not a game where the Storyteller is trying to beat the players. You are creating room for chaos and letting the chaos take the table in various directions that will result in fun outcomes. The players dying is the fate of the dice, not the objective for running the game.

Richard Moore

How to Read This Script

  • Boxes marked “Read aloud” are for the players. Read them word for word.
  • Gray, smaller text is for you alone. It tells you what to ask, what to roll and what to write down.
  • The Places has a page for every place in town. When the party goes somewhere, turn to that page.
  • When It Happens has a box for everything the dice can do, from a Cost or Trouble to a chase, a capture, a Tell or the final flight. When one of these happens, turn to its box and read it.
  • The rules here are the rulebook’s, written in plain words, so the book agrees with them. If a question comes up that this script doesn’t answer, make a quick ruling and keep the game moving.

What You Need

  • Each player: one each of d4, d6, d8, d10 and d12, plus a second d6 (the Mask) and a second d10 (the Monster), each in a different color from the trait dice. A 0 on a d10 counts as 10. Players can share if you’re short.
  • You: a d6 (for Tells and chases) and this script.
  • The Entity cards, one per Entity, from The Eight Entities near the end. The rulebook’s defaults are already chosen. Print the cards and cut them out so the players can choose. The Castle Duty can be changed at the table (Part One).
  • The tracker on the last page, printed out. It keeps track of Suspicion, the Turns, the Lead, the shopping list and the lock-up, with a coin or a token on each track.
  • The map of Hallowby (Hallowby at a Glance). Print that page a second time, larger if you can, and put it where everyone can see it. Players move a token or a coin to show where their Entity is.

The Evening at a Glance

20–25 minPart One: The Castle. Who the players are, the shopping list, choosing Entities and Castle Duties, and a practice roll.
45–90 minPart Two: Into Hallowby. The raid: twelve Turns of sneaking, rolling and maybe running.
0–15 minThe final flight, if the town turns out with pitchforks.
5–10 minPart Three: How the Year Went. What came home, and how the castle’s year goes.

If your group wants a break, take it after Part One, right before the raid.

The Night in Numbers

Hallowby is an Easy town. Keep these numbers handy; they’re on the tracker too.

  • The shopping list: 4 items. One is essential, the cask of red wine.
  • Turns: 12, then dawn. Charges: 3 for each Entity.
  • Suspicion: starts at 0. The Limit is 9, or 8 with five players. When Suspicion reaches it, the whole town hunts the party in the final flight.
  • The way out: Difficulty 6. The lock-up: Difficulty 10.
  • A local chase: the Lead starts at 1, escape at 4, against a mob of 8 plus half the Suspicion when the chase starts (rounded down, at most 11). The chase raises Suspicion once, when it ends. It lasts 6 rounds at most.
  • The final flight: the Lead starts at 2, escape at 5, against a mob of 10.

Running a Turn

The raid is twelve Turns. Run each Turn like this (it’s on the tracker too).

  1. Say the Turn, as in “Turn 3.” The marker moves at the end of each Turn, in step 5.
  2. Go around the table. Ask each player, “What does your Entity do?” Each takes one action, which is to roll at the next obstacle where it is, move to another place, pick up dropped items, or wait. They act one at a time, in any order they like, and each result counts at once.
  3. A move takes the Turn, and the Entity is there now. Turn to that place’s page and read its arrival box. If it’s a watched place that nobody has reached yet (for the way out, the first time anyone comes back to it; at the lock-up, a captive’s arrival doesn’t count), make a Tell check once everyone moving there this Turn has arrived. If you’re not sure who else is coming, ask before the next roll (When It Happens).
  4. A roll. Ask which trait, Mask or Monster, and whether anyone spends a charge. Then they roll, you read the result and you mark any Suspicion. One roll raises Suspicion once, by its biggest trigger (an overdraw adds its +2 on top).
  5. End of the Turn. If the mirror has been taken and is still in town, Suspicion +1. Then move the Turn marker. If that was Turn 12, dawn comes (When It Happens).

Whenever Suspicion reaches the Limit, finish the roll’s result first, then stop wherever you are and turn to The Final Flight. If that roll got the party out of town, they’re out and nobody runs. If it got someone caught, there’s no local chase, and they run in the final flight with everyone else.

Keeping it moving

  • Let the players plan out loud, but if a Turn stalls, ask the quietest player what they do.
  • The party can split up. Hallowby is small enough to go around together, but splitting up saves Turns.
  • When a player asks “Can I…?”, the answer is usually “Yes, if you get past the obstacle.” Every place is in this script, and nothing else needs a roll.
  • Anyone with nothing to do, such as a player waiting at the bridge or in the lock-up, can play a villager, like Granny Mott, the constable or a costume contest judge.
  • Give the villagers voices and keep the rules talk short. The script keeps track of the rules so you don’t have to.
