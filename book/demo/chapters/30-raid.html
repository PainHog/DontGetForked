Part Two

Into Hallowby

About 75–100 minutes, the final flight included. Read the next two boxes, then start Turn 1.

The Edge of Town

The path comes out of the woods at the bottom of the hill, by a little humpbacked bridge. That bridge is your way out: when it’s time to go home, you all meet back here.

Across it, Hallowby is in full swing. Turnip lanterns grin from every window. A fiddle band is murdering a jig in the square. Children dressed as bats chase children dressed as ghosts. An old woman with a lantern and a pitchfork, “for the parade”, gives you a long look, and decides you’re in costume.

You’re in.

How the Night Works

The night lasts 12 Turns. When the twelfth is over, the sun comes up.

Each Turn, each of you does one thing: roll to get past something, move to another place, pick up something that was dropped, or wait. You go one at a time, in any order you like, and everything happens straight away. You can split up.

Moving anywhere on the map takes one Turn. There are the four places on the list, the lock-up, and the bridge.

Every place has obstacles: a locked door, a guard dog, a nosy shopkeeper. You get past them in order, and whoever gets past the last one has the item. Each obstacle tells you which traits work and how hard it is.

Every place has two ways in. The first time any of you tries a place, you choose one, and that’s your way in there for the rest of the night.

Once one of you gets past an obstacle, it stays beaten for everybody. The exception is a crowd: everyone gets past a crowd for themselves.

Some obstacles have a loud way. It works, but it puts Suspicion up by 1, whatever you roll.

Some obstacles are watched. Get Trouble at a watched one, and you’re caught: you run for it. If the crowd corners you, you go in the lock-up, and the others can get you out.

You can hand things to each other any time you’re in the same place, and you can carry as much as you like. Except the mirror: that’s furniture, and furniture is heavy.

When you’re done, everyone who isn’t locked up meets at the bridge, and one of you rolls to get you all out of town.

And remember Suspicion. If it reaches 11, or you’re still in town when the sun comes up, the whole town hunts you, and you run for the castle together. That’s the final flight. That’s how monsters get forked.

Then begin.

It’s Turn 1. Where do you go?

If they can’t decide: the tavern cellar is a gentle first stop, since nothing there is watched, and it has the essential. Turn to The Places for wherever they go.

Your first roll: walk them through it

  1. The first time anyone tries a place: “Which way in?” Read both, then they choose.
  2. “Which trait?” Only a trait the obstacle lists, unless an ability says otherwise. Read them the choices.
  3. “Mask or Monster?”
  4. “Is anyone spending a charge?” Before the roll: theirs, or a friend’s at the same place.
  5. Castle Duty: if this place has the item for the roller’s Duty, their trait die is one size bigger (a d8 becomes a d10; a d12 stays a d12). That’s the roll’s one bigger die: no ability can add another.
  6. Roll and add, and compare with the Difficulty: a Success, a Cost (1–2 short) or Trouble (3 or more short). Doubles on a Success: a Critical.
  7. Suspicion: the loud way +1, Trouble +1, the Monster shows +2. One roll raises it once, by its biggest: the loud way and the Monster showing make +2, not +3.
  8. Read the result’s line from the obstacle. A Cost or Trouble: turn to When It Happens.

What Raises Suspicion

Trouble+1
The Monster shows+2
Taking the loud way+1, whatever the result
A Cost picked as Suspicion+1
Overdraw+2
A Tell goes off+1 (not from a roll)
The mirror, once taken+1 at the end of every Turn, even set down, until it leaves town, is lost or is abandoned

One roll raises Suspicion only once, by its biggest trigger, overdraw included. Good results never lower it. It never goes past 11: when it reaches 11, the final flight starts at once.
