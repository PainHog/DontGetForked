Part Two

The Places

One page for each place on the map. When anyone arrives, read the arrival box. The first time anyone tries a location, read both ways in and let them choose; a try by a way of an Entity’s own picks one too. They keep that way in for the night, so after that, just say which way the party took. Each obstacle has its own box. Read it, then the roll line, then the line for the result. For a Cost, Trouble or anything else the dice do, turn to When It Happens. Each obstacle’s Success line is for the quiet way; where the loud way was taken, read its own line if it has one. When an ability opened a way of its own, say how it went in a line of your own. On a later arrival, leave out anything already taken.

1 · The Tavern Cellar

The cask of red wine (essential) · food and drink, so the Cook’s trait die is one size bigger here · nothing here is watched, so no Tell check and nobody gets caught here.

The Grinning Turnip is the loudest building in Hallowby tonight, all fiddles and singing, with a queue out of the door. Nobody, of course, is looking round the back.

Behind the tavern, a muddy yard full of geese leads to the kitchen door. In the alley beside it, a heavy trapdoor drops to the cellar. Which way?

Way in · The goose yard

The yard is ankle-deep in mud and full of geese. Geese are the worst guards in the world, or the best, depending which side of them you’re on.

Sly to tiptoe through. Or Nimble, the loud way: run for it, honking. Difficulty 6. Not watched.

Success: You cross the whole yard without a single honk. The kitchen door is on the latch, and the cellar steps are right inside.

Success, the loud way: You charge through in a storm of feathers. Every goose in Hallowby knows your face now, but you’re in.

Trouble: One goose sees you, and then, as geese do, all of them see you. You back out of the yard, covered in mud, to a chorus of honking. (Suspicion +1. Nobody is watching, so no chase.)

or Way in · The cellar trapdoor

In the alley, an oak trapdoor, iron-bound and as heavy as a coffin lid.

Brawn. Difficulty 8. Not watched.

Success: It lifts with a groan nobody hears over the fiddles. Steps lead down into the dark.

Trouble: It won’t budge, and something in your back goes ping. (Suspicion +1. No chase.)

Then · The back room

The cellar is one dark, cluttered back room, full of barrels and sacks and a broken mangle and forty years of things nobody threw away. The wine is in here somewhere.

Wits to work out which barrel. Or Sly, the loud way: rummage through everything. Difficulty 8. Not watched.

Success: There, behind the pickled onions, is a small cask with a faded red label. For the guests.

Trouble: You bring down a shelf of saucepans. Upstairs, the fiddles stop. There’s a long pause, and then, thank goodness, they start up again. (Suspicion +1. No chase.)

Whoever got past the back room is holding the cask of red wine, and the essential is yours.

Mark the wine on the tracker, with who carries it.

2 · The Florist

The pot of nightshade · plants and seeds, so the Gardener’s trait die is one size bigger here · watched, so the first time anyone arrives, make a Tell check once everyone moving here this Turn has arrived (When It Happens).

Mrs Thorne, the florist, is selling festival garlands as fast as she can wrap them. The shop floor is packed with people in costume, and from behind the till she sees every one of them.

The nightshade sits on a high shelf at the back. Up the side wall, a rickety drainpipe runs to an open window just above that shelf. Which way?

Way in · The crowded shop floor group · watched

Wall-to-wall witches, skeletons and one very hot man in a bear suit, all queuing for garlands.

Sly to slip through the crowd. Difficulty 8. Watched. It’s a crowd: each of you who wants past rolls for yourself. Whoever gets through takes the pot; if several get through at once, you choose who.

Success: A bit of elbow, a lot of “pardon me”, and the nightshade is in your hands.

Trouble: Someone treads on your foot. You yelp, in a voice that isn’t quite human, and the whole shop turns round. “Thief!” (Suspicion +1, and caught in a local chase.)

If two or more roll the crowd in the same Turn, that’s a group check (When It Happens). A Ghost with its Spectral Perk isn’t stopped by the crowd. It walks through with no roll and no action, even in the Turn it arrives, and takes the pot (unless it’s already carrying something).

or Way in · The drainpipe

The alley beside the shop is empty. The drainpipe is held to the wall with three rusty brackets and a prayer.

Nimble to climb it. Difficulty 10. Not watched.

Success: Up, in through the window, and the nightshade is right there on the shelf.

Trouble: The drainpipe comes away from the wall with you still on it, and you land in a water butt. (Suspicion +1. No chase.)

Whoever got through has the pot of nightshade, purely ornamental, of course.

3 · The Carpenter

Hinges that creak properly · tools and hardware, so the Handyman’s trait die is one size bigger here · watched, with a Tell check on the first arrival.

The carpenter’s workshop is shut for the festival. The lane outside is busy with families heading for the square, and every few minutes the night watchman’s lantern comes bobbing past on his round.

The front door is locked. Round the back, a high garden wall hides the timber yard. Through the window you can see the hinges, hanging on a board above the workbench. Which way?

Way in · The front door watched

A stout front door with a good lock, in full view of the lane.

Sly to pick the lock. Or Brawn, the loud way: kick it in. Difficulty 8. Watched.

Success: Click. The door swings open on hinges that, tragically, don’t creak at all.

Success, the loud way: One kick and the door is in, with you right behind it.

Trouble: The lock holds, and a man dressed as a pumpkin asks what you think you’re doing. (Suspicion +1, and caught in a local chase.)

or Way in · The garden wall

A high brick wall with broken glass along the top, and a padlocked gate.

Nimble to climb it. Or Brawn, the loud way: through the gate, lock and all. Difficulty 8. Not watched.

Success: You drop over the wall among the timber stacks, and would you believe it, the back door isn’t even locked.

Success, the loud way: The gate, lock and all, lies flat among the timber stacks.

Trouble: Halfway up, the top of the wall comes away in your hands. (Suspicion +1. No chase.)

Trouble, the loud way: The gate, lock and all, doesn’t budge. (Suspicion +1. No chase.)

Then · The night watchman watched

You’re inside, but the workbench is right under the front window, and here comes the watchman’s lantern again.

Wits to time his round. Difficulty 8. Watched.

Success: The lantern swings left, then right, and now you go. By the time he looks in, the board is empty and so is the room.

Trouble: He comes back early, and the lantern swings round to you. “Oi!” (Suspicion +1, and caught in a local chase.)

Whoever got past the watchman has the hinges. Hear how beautifully they creak?

4 · The Tailor

A new cape · cloth and costumes, so the Tailor’s trait die is one size bigger here, the guard dog included · watched, with a Tell check on the first arrival · the mirror is here.

The tailor’s shop is lit up like a stage. Mrs Pinch, the tailor, stands behind her counter with a mouthful of pins, ready for last-minute repairs. The costume contest judges, a gang of very serious children, are due any minute.

The new cape hangs on a stand behind her. The side window is shuttered. And at the back of the shop, beside a tall gilt mirror, an enormous dog is asleep, at least for now. Which way?

Way in · Mrs Pinch at the counter watched

Mrs Pinch looks you up and down, and takes the pins out of her mouth.

Charm to talk her round. Or Sly, the loud way: snatch it from under her nose. Difficulty 8. Watched.

Success: You need it for the costume contest, you explain. She is so pleased someone appreciates a good cape that she wraps it for you.

Success, the loud way: You snatch the cape and you’re gone before the pins hit the floor.

Trouble: She peers at you over her spectacles. “That’s not a costume, dear,” she says. “That’s a face.” (Suspicion +1, and caught in a local chase.)

or Way in · The shuttered window

A side window in a dark passage, its shutters latched on the inside.

Nimble to slip the catch and climb in. Or Brawn, the loud way: force the shutters. Difficulty 10. Not watched.

Success: The catch lifts with a fingernail. In, out, and the cape is over your arm.

Success, the loud way: The shutters give with a bang, and you’re in.

Trouble: The shutter swings back and bangs like a gunshot. Mrs Pinch drops her pins. (Suspicion +1, but no chase, because nobody saw who.)

Whoever got in has the new cape.

The Mirror

Once the cape has been taken, anyone at the tailor’s may try for the mirror (if the way in was a way only one of them could take, only that one). Taking it, once they’re past the dog, is free. Before anyone takes it, count aloud. From the Turn it’s taken, it adds Suspicion +1 at the end of every Turn until it leaves town, usually 2 in all if it goes straight to the bridge, so warn the players if that would reach 9.

Then · The guard dog watched · for the mirror

The dog is the size of a small pony, and it’s lying across the mirror’s feet. As you watch, one eye opens.

Charm: a biscuit and a pat. Or Nimble, the loud way: outrun it, barking. Difficulty 10 (it’s guarding the furniture). Watched.

Success: The dog thumps its tail twice, rolls over, and lets you have the mirror.

Success, the loud way: You bolt past with the dog at your heels; by the time it skids to a stop, the mirror is yours.

Trouble: The dog wakes up all at once, and all of it barks. (Suspicion +1, and caught in a local chase.)

Read this when someone takes the mirror. If it’s the Creature, leave out “your Nimble is one size smaller”: its Tireless Perk keeps its Nimble the same size.

The mirror is Bulky, which means one of you can carry it. While you do, you can’t use the Mask, your Nimble is one size smaller, and every move takes your action in two Turns in a row. After the first you’re somewhere in between, and you arrive when you act in the second.

And a mirror that size gets noticed. From now on, Suspicion goes up by 1 at the end of every Turn until it’s out of town. You can put it down any time, except when you’ve been caught and are running for it, but only giving it up for good stops the noise.

If Dracula is in the shop, the mirror shows the counter, the dog, the biscuit, and everybody except him (flavour only). Taking the mirror back up after it’s set down is an action. Abandoned, it stays where it is, makes no more noise, and can’t be taken again.

The Lock-up

Always watched. The first time anyone arrives who isn’t a captive, make a Tell check. A captive’s arrival doesn’t count. For rescue and slipping free, see When It Happens.

The lock-up is a squat stone hut behind the square, with one barred door, one barred window, and a constable asleep in a chair outside with his hat over his eyes.

The Way Out

Always watched. The first time anyone comes back here, make a Tell check. Only when everyone who isn’t locked up is here may one of them roll.

The humpbacked bridge at the edge of town. Families are crossing it with lanterns, heading home; beyond it the path climbs into the woods and up to the castle.

If anyone is in the lock-up, read this before anyone rolls.

Anyone still in the lock-up when you cross is left behind. Are you sure you’re all here?

The way out watched

One of you rolls for everybody. Sly to slip across with the crowd, or Nimble to go along under the bridge. Or Brawn, the loud way: barge straight through. Difficulty 6. Watched.

Success or Cost: You’re across, and the woods close behind you. (The Cost here costs nothing. Even if this roll takes Suspicion to 9, they’re out, and nobody runs. Turn to Part Three.)

Trouble: A dad with a lantern lifts it high, right into your face, and screams. (Suspicion +1, and the roller is caught in a local chase. The way out can be rolled only once a Turn, so the party tries again next Turn.)
