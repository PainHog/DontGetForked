Part One

The Castle

About 20–25 minutes. Have the Entity cards face up in the middle of the table, the map of Hallowby where everyone can see it, and the tracker beside you, set to Suspicion 0 and Turn 1. When everyone is settled, begin.

Welcome

Tonight is Halloween, and you’re playing monsters, the real kind.

You all live together in an old castle in the woods above a small town called Hallowby. The castle is out of almost everything it needs, and the only way to restock is to take it from the town.

Once a year, Hallowby holds a festival called Lantern Night. Everyone in town wears a costume and carries a lantern through the streets until sunrise. It’s the one night a real monster can walk through town without anyone looking twice.

You have until sunrise to steal everything on the castle’s shopping list and bring it home. If the town gets too suspicious, everyone comes after you with pitchforks and you have to run for the castle. If they catch you, you get forked.

The Shopping List

Point to the four places on the map as you read each item.

The castle’s shopping list is pinned to the kitchen door. There are four things on it.

  • A cask of red wine, from the cellar of the tavern. This one is essential, because without it the castle can’t have a good year.
  • A pot of nightshade, from the florist.
  • A set of new hinges, from the carpenter.
  • A new cape, from the tailor.

At the bottom, someone has added, “A mirror would be nice too.”

If you bring home the wine and miss no more than one of the other three, that’s a good year, a Win. If you do all that and also bring home a piece of furniture, it’s a Grand Year. There’s a gold-framed mirror at the tailor’s.

Choosing Your Monster

Read the eight introductions, pointing to each card. Then let the players choose.

Eight monsters live in the castle. Each of you plays one, and no two of you can play the same one.

  • Dracula is the best talker in the castle. He can look a guard in the eye and walk right past, but he can’t stand garlic.
  • Frankenstein’s Creature is the strongest. It breaks the locks others would pick, and it carries furniture better than anyone.
  • The Mummy is the smartest. It’s three thousand years old and remembers every trick there is.
  • The Werewolf is the fastest. When the wolf shows, the crowd just sees a very large dog.
  • The Invisible Man is the sneakiest. Nobody can catch what they can’t see, unless he’s carrying something.
  • A Ghost drifts through walls and slips past crowds, but it can’t stand cold iron.
  • A Witch is a good friend to have nearby, because her spell can make anyone’s roll better. Her black cat warns her when someone is watching.
  • Jekyll & Hyde is two monsters in one body. One is a kind doctor, and the other is a brute who comes out at the worst times.

Each of you, pick one and take its card.

If two players want the same Entity, they roll off (again on a tie): the high d6 keeps it, and the other chooses again. Once everyone has a card, ask each player in turn to say their Entity’s name, so everyone knows who’s who. If asked: the Werewolf’s very large dog costs a charge. Tonight’s mirror is Bulky, so anyone can carry it, and the Creature’s Tireless means carrying it doesn’t make its Nimble smaller.

Your Card

Look at your card. Every monster has five traits: Brawn for force and lifting, Nimble for climbing and running, Sly for sneaking and stealing, Charm for talking and passing as human, and Wits for noticing things and figuring them out.

Each trait is a die. You all have the same five dice, a d4, d6, d8, d10 and d12, in a different order. Your d12 is what you’re best at, and your d4 is what you’d rather leave to someone else.

Every time you roll, you add a second die, and you choose which one. If you pick the Mask, a d6, you keep your costume on and pass as human. If you pick the Monster, a d10, you let the monster out. The Monster is stronger, but if its number comes up higher than your trait die’s, the town sees what you really are. That raises Suspicion, the whole town’s alarm, by 2.

Suspicion is one track that you all share. When it reaches 9 (8 if there are five of you), the whole town comes after you, so try to keep it low.

A Practice Roll

Nothing in this practice counts. There’s no Suspicion and no charges, and Jekyll stays Jekyll. Ask each player to roll their d12 trait with the Mask d6, add them, and tell you the total. Read the box as far as the Critical, tell each player their result, then read its last paragraph.

Every roll is against a number called the Difficulty, which is a 6, 8, 10 or 12. The bigger it is, the harder the roll. For this one, let’s say it’s 8.

  • 8 or more is a Success. You do it.
  • 1 or 2 short, a 6 or a 7, is a Cost. You still do it, but something goes wrong, and I pick what it is.
  • 3 or more short is Trouble. You don’t do it, and Suspicion goes up. If someone was watching, you’re caught and you have to run.
  • And if both dice show the same number on a Success, that’s a Critical, and something extra good happens.

Now roll the d12 again, this time with the Monster d10 instead of the Mask. If the d10 came up higher than the d12, the Monster showed. The Monster usually helps you succeed, but every time it shows tonight, Suspicion goes up by 2. The Mask never shows.

When everyone has rolled, go on.

Your Powers

Each of you has 3 charges. One charge pays for one use of an ability, either your Signature or your Gift, and both are on your card. Spend the charge before you roll. An ability never gets you past an obstacle on its own; you still have to roll.

Almost every ability does one of four things: it makes your trait die one size bigger, lets you roll a different trait, lets you roll the Monster without raising Suspicion, or opens a way past an obstacle that only you can take. Your card says which. Jekyll’s Draught is the one exception, because it changes his form. And when the town is chasing you, any ability can make your trait die one size bigger instead.

You can also spend a charge on a friend’s roll if you’re in the same place. It uses your charge but not your action. Only one thing can make a die bigger on any one roll, and if something also makes it smaller, the two cancel out.

If you run out of charges, you can still use an ability. That’s called an overdraw, and instead of a charge it costs Suspicion +2, on top of anything else the roll raises.

Your Perk is always on, and it’s free. Your Weakness is something the mob brings along when it chases you, and your Tell is the small thing that gives you away. Tonight, keep the Gift and Perk on your card.

Castle Duties

One last thing. Everyone at the castle has a job, called a Castle Duty, and does the shopping for it. At the place where your Duty’s item is, your trait die is one size bigger on your own rolls.

Tonight’s list needs a Cook for the wine, a Gardener for the nightshade, a Handyman for the hinges and a Tailor for the cape. The Butler and the Librarian have nothing to buy tonight.

Your card already has a Duty. You can keep it or switch to any Duty nobody else has, but no two of you can have the same one.

If two players want the same Duty, they roll off (again on a tie): the high d6 keeps it, and the other picks another. Write each player’s Duty on the tracker. The six Duties are Cook, Gardener, Librarian, Butler, Handyman and Tailor.

Setting Off

The castle door creaks open. Down the hill, through the trees, you can see Hallowby glowing. There’s a turnip lantern in every window, and music is coming from the square.

You check your costumes one last time and start down the path to town.
