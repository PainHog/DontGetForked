Storyteller’s Guide

How to run a raid, on one page. The chapter beside each heading has the full rule.

Before the Game Chapters 1, 8

  • Print an Entity Sheet and At the Table for each player, The Eight (Chapter 2) and the town’s page (Chapter 9), or roll a town (Chapter 8, about half an hour). Track Suspicion, the Turns and the Lead.
  • Dice: each player a d4, d6, d8, d10 and d12, plus a Mask d6 and a Monster d10 in another colour. You: a d20, a d10 and two d6.
  • 3–5 players, 1–2 hours. Choose Easy, Standard or Hard:

Setup Chapters 2, 4

  1. Roll the shopping list and the furniture (or use the town’s) and read them out before anyone picks.
  2. Each player picks an Entity, then a Gift, Perk and Castle Duty (defaults marked). Two who want the same Entity, or the same Duty, roll off: the high d6 keeps it. A random Duty that’s taken or not on the list is rolled again while a useful one remains.
  3. 3 charges each, Suspicion 0, and everyone starts at the way out. Entering town needs no roll.

Running a Turn Chapter 4

  1. Say the Turn (“Turn 3”). Warn when three are left.
  2. Go round the table: each Entity takes one action (roll, move, pick up or wait), one at a time, in any order, each finished, chase and all, before the next. If the hunt starts mid-Turn, the rest don’t act.
  3. A move: they’re there. A watched place’s first arrival gets a Tell check.
  4. A roll: ask the trait, Mask or Monster, and who spends charges. Mark Suspicion once, by the biggest trigger (an overdraw adds +2 on top).
  5. End of the Turn: furniture taken and still in town, Suspicion +1. Move the Turn marker. When Turn 12 ends, dawn: anyone in town flees.

When to Roll What Chapters 3–5

  • Players roll only at obstacles (the way out and the lock-up too) and in chases.
  • Tell check: the first time anyone reaches a watched location (the lock-up too, not a captive’s arrival; the way out once anyone comes back), when all moving there this Turn have arrived (ask first who else is coming). On a d6 of 4–6, pick an arrival with a d6 and describe its Tell: Suspicion +1.
  • Group check: several at one group obstacle in a Turn declare dice and abilities, then roll at once; each passes on its own result. One rise for the check; all with Trouble are caught together.
  • Costs: pick the one that hurts most now (a lost Turn near dawn, a smaller die before a big roll, a dropped item but never what this roll wins or furniture, Suspicion +1 near the Limit), but never a cost that costs nothing, like Suspicion +1 on a roll that already raised it. At the way out a Cost costs nothing. Say what happened.
  • Trouble at an unwatched obstacle: Suspicion +1, no chase.

Running a Local Chase Chapter 6

  1. Set the mob when it starts: 8 + half the Suspicion (after the roll’s rise), rounded down, at most 11, for the whole chase. The Lead starts at 1.
  2. Each round, roll the ground (d6, below) and describe it. Once it’s read, before anyone rolls, a runner may give up: captured, no roll.
  3. Weakness: Always from round 1, Soon from round 3: the trait die one size smaller to the end. Make it a moment.
  4. The runner picks a trait from the ground, and Mask or Monster (no Mask carrying furniture). Abilities on its own roll only, any as a raise; one overdraw a chase.
  5. The Lead: Success +1 (Critical +2), Cost 0, Trouble −1. Caught together: one Lead, by the majority rule. A Cost brings no Storyteller’s Cost.
  6. Lead 4: escaped, back where caught, Turn used. Lead 0: cornered, captured. After round 6: a Lead of 2+ escapes, less is cornered.
  7. One rise, when it ends: Trouble +1 or the Monster +2, the bigger, plus +2 per overdraw. Given up before anyone rolled: nothing.

Captured and Rescued Chapter 6

Captured, the town takes what the runner carried, furniture too, for the night. The lock-up is one move from anywhere, watched, Difficulty 10.

  • Rescue (by anyone not held, at the lock-up): Sly, or Brawn the loud way. A Success or a Cost frees every captive there; Trouble, Suspicion +1 and the rescuer is caught.
  • Slip free (the captive’s action, once a Turn from the Turn after its capture): Sly or Nimble, or Brawn the loud way. A Success frees it; a Cost does nothing; Trouble, Suspicion +1, no chase.
  • The freed act next Turn, from the lock-up. Let a waiting player voice a jailer or a villager.

The Limit and the Hunt Chapter 5

A roll’s result comes first: if the roll that reaches the Limit gets the party out, they’re out, and captives it frees join the flight. Trouble that brings the Limit starts no local chase: the roller flees with everyone. If a chase’s rise brings it, an escaped runner joins the flight; anyone cornered was captured first. Then Suspicion stops; extra points are lost. Everyone held at once: the night ends, Forked.

Running the Final Flight Chapter 6

Everyone not captured flees together, from wherever they are. Lead 2, escape at 5 (6 on Hard), mob 10 (Easy) or 11.

  • Each round, roll the ground; everyone rolls, Monster only, in any order. Abilities on anyone’s roll, any as a raise; still one raise a roll.
  • Majority rule: Successes (a Critical is two) against Trouble. Ahead by 1, the Lead moves 1; by 2+, 2; level, it stays.
  • A new chase: count Weakness rounds from 1 again. Overdraw: no Suspicion, but a step down from the next roll to the end, on top of any Weakness; once a flight.
  • Escape: home with what they carry; captives left behind. Cornered: Forked.

Play the mob like the weather: always there, always closer.

Ending Chapter 7

An item is home if an Entity who got out carries it. Grand Year: a Win and the furniture. Win: every essential, at most one extra missing. Partial: at least half the list (a missing essential makes it Partial at best). Bust: less. Forked: cornered in the flight, or everyone held. Then one step down for each Entity left behind (never below Bust). Read the epilogue: the result’s line, then (unless Forked) one for each kind of item missing.

Quick Advice

  • You’re not out to beat them. Make room for chaos; a forking is the dice’s doing.
  • Make a ruling and keep moving. Look it up after the game.
  • Describe the town. Everyone’s in costume and sees what they expect. Give watched obstacles a face (Chapter 8’s villagers), and let the mood follow Suspicion: whispers, then pointing, then torches.
  • Let them laugh at the Monster showing, then mark the +2.
  • Pace it. Steer a new player to a Difficulty 8 first. Warn the party if carried furniture would reach the Limit. Near the Limit, remind a caught runner that giving up before anyone rolls raises nothing.
