Part Two

The Final Flight

When Suspicion reaches the Limit, or dawn comes with anyone still in town. Stop wherever you are, even in the middle of a Turn. If it was a local chase’s rise that brought it, anyone who escaped that chase runs too, and anyone cornered in it, or who gave up, is in the lock-up. Captives stay in the lock-up. If every Entity is in the lock-up, nobody runs. Turn to Part Three, where the year is Forked.

Hallowby has had enough. The bells are ringing, the music has stopped, and every lantern in town is turning toward you. The whole town is coming after you with pitchforks and brooms, and old Granny Mott is at the front.

Run for the castle, all of you, together, right now.

This is the final flight. Everyone who isn’t locked up runs together, from wherever you are.

Your Lead starts at 2. Get it to 5 and you’re home. If it drops to 0, they’ve got you, and you’re forked.

The masks are off now. Everyone rolls the Monster, and Suspicion doesn’t matter anymore. Every round I roll the ground, and it gives you two traits to choose from. Each of you rolls one of them against the mob, and you can spend charges on each other’s rolls. Any ability can now be spent just to make someone’s trait die one size bigger, whatever it usually does, even if your card says it never works in a chase.

Then we count. If you have more Successes than Trouble, your Lead goes up by 1, or by 2 if you’re two or more ahead. If you have more Trouble than Successes, it goes down the same way. If they’re tied, it stays the same. A Cost counts for neither, and a Critical counts as two Successes.

Anything you drop now stays in Hallowby.

Running It

  1. Who runs: every Entity who isn’t captured. The Lead starts at 2. Escape at 5. Cornered at 0 means Forked.
  2. The mob’s Difficulty is 10, however many run. Suspicion has stopped, and nothing raises it now.
  3. Each round: roll a d6 on the ground table (When It Happens) and read its line. Each runner picks one of its two traits. The Monster d10 only. Carrying the mirror makes Nimble one size smaller (not the Creature’s: it’s Tireless). Abilities may go on anyone’s roll, and any ability may be spent as a raise instead of doing what it usually does (one raise per roll). Everyone rolls, in any order the party likes.
  4. The majority rule: count the Successes (a Critical counts as two) and the Trouble; Costs count for neither. If Successes are ahead by 1, the Lead goes up 1, and by 2 or more, it goes up 2. If Trouble is ahead, it goes down the same way. A tie means no change. For example, a Success, a Critical, a Cost and a Trouble make 3 Successes to 1 Trouble, so the Lead goes up 2.
  5. The Weakness: the flight is a new chase for everyone, even someone who came straight from a local chase. From round 1 for Dracula and a Ghost, from round 3 for everyone else, the trait die is one size smaller to the end of the flight. Read its line. A bigger die from an ability cancels it for that roll.
  6. Overdraw now costs a smaller die instead of Suspicion. From the overdrawer’s own next roll to the end of the flight, its trait die is one size smaller, on top of any Weakness. Once per flight each, Weakness or not.

Home

The Lead reaches 5. Everyone in the flight is home, with everything they carry. Anyone still in the lock-up is left behind.

The last pitchfork falls behind in the woods, and the castle door slams shut behind you. Far below, you can still hear the town shouting.

You made it. Let’s see what you brought home.

Turn to Part Three.

Forked

The Lead reaches 0. The year’s result is Forked, whatever the party carries. Turn to Part Three and read only the Forked line.

They’ve cornered you at the bottom of the hill, with the castle’s windows glowing just above the trees. The lanterns close in, and the pitchforks come up.

You got forked.
