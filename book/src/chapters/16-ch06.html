Chapter 6

Chases and Getting Forked

There are two kinds of chase. Caught during the raid, you run from a crowd in a local chase. When Suspicion reaches the Limit, or dawn comes with anyone still in town, the whole town turns out with pitchforks for the final flight.

The Lead

A chase is played in rounds, on a Lead track. The Lead starts low, and you try to raise it to the escape number before the mob brings it to 0. The starting Lead, the escape number and the mob’s Difficulty depend on the kind of chase (below). Each round:

  1. The ground. The Storyteller rolls a d6 on the chase table below. The row names two traits that work this round, for everyone in the chase.
  2. Choose. Each Entity in the chase picks one of those two traits, and its second die. In a local chase that is the Mask or the Monster (no Mask while carrying furniture). In the final flight it is the Monster only.
  3. Abilities. Spend them before rolling, as usual. An ability that changes the trait rolls its trait instead of the ground’s. Opening an approach never works in a chase, and Castle Duty gives no raise.
  4. Roll against the mob’s Difficulty.
  5. Move the Lead. Fleeing alone, your result moves it.
    • Success: Lead +1 (a Critical: +2).
    • Cost: no change.
    • Trouble: Lead −1.
    Fleeing with others, it moves once for the round, by the majority rule (below).

Reach the escape number and you’re clear. Reach 0 and you’re cornered, which means captured in a local chase or forked in the final flight.

A chase roll moves the Lead instead of getting you past an obstacle. A Cost brings no Storyteller’s Cost, and Trouble starts no new chase.

One chase, one rise. A local chase raises Suspicion once, when it ends, by the biggest trigger among all its rolls, however many rounds it took and whoever rolled them: Trouble +1, the Monster showing +2. An overdraw in the chase adds its +2 on top. Until the chase ends, Suspicion stays where it was. In the final flight it has already stopped.

Your Weakness

Once the mob brings your Weakness, your trait die is one size smaller to the end of the chase. Each Weakness says when the mob brings it.

  • Always: from the first round of any chase (Dracula, a Ghost).
  • Soon: from the third round of any chase (everyone else).

Count rounds from 1 in each chase. The final flight is a new chase, even for an Entity who joins it from a local chase. In the final flight, an overdraw can bring your Weakness in sooner (more on that below).

The Local Chase

When Trouble at a watched obstacle gets you caught, you run for it. If that Trouble brings the Limit, there’s no local chase: you flee with everyone else in the final flight.

  • Lead: starts at 1. You escape at 4.
  • The mob’s Difficulty: 8 plus half the Suspicion when the chase starts, rounded down, at most 12. It stays the same for the whole chase. (Suspicion 5: 8 + 2 = 10.)
  • Abilities work on your own roll only. Nobody else can spend a charge on your roll in a local chase, not even a fellow runner.
  • You can’t drop or hand over what you carry until the chase ends.
  • The whole chase, however many rounds it takes, happens inside the Turn it started. Escape, and you’re back where you were caught, with that Turn used up.
  • Entities caught together in the same group check flee together on one shared Lead, moved by the majority rule (below), and are captured together if cornered.
  • Giving up. At the start of any round, a runner may give up and be captured, with no roll. Anyone caught with it carries on without it.
  • If the chase’s rise brings Suspicion to the Limit, the hunt is on as soon as the chase ends. A runner who escaped joins the final flight; anyone cornered, or who gave up, was captured first and stays behind.

Captured

Cornered in a local chase, you are captured, and the town takes back what you were carrying, furniture included. It’s gone for the night, and nobody can take it again. You are held at the town’s lock-up. Like a location, it is a place of its own, one move from anywhere unless the map says otherwise, and always watched.

While held, you can’t pick up or be handed anything. You can still spend charges on any roll at the lock-up (your own, a rescuer’s or another captive’s).

Left behind. Anyone still held when the others leave town, by the way out or by escaping the final flight, is left behind (see How the Year Went, Chapter 7).

Everyone held. If every Entity in the party is held at once, the night ends there and then. The town has the lot of you, and the year is Forked.

The Final Flight

At the Limit, or at dawn with anyone still in town, every Entity who isn’t captured flees together, wherever they are.

  • Lead: starts at 2. The party escapes at 5 (6 on Hard).
  • The mob’s Difficulty: 10 on Easy, 11 on Standard and Hard, however many flee.
  • Your second die is the Monster, because the Mask is off.
  • Abilities can go on anyone’s roll, since you all flee together. Any ability may also be spent as a raise (the trait die one size bigger) instead of doing what it usually does. Still one raise per roll.
  • The Lead moves by the majority rule (below).
  • Anything you drop in the flight is left in town.

The Majority Rule

Whenever several Entities share a chase (the final flight, or a local chase begun by one group check), everyone in it rolls each round, in any order the party likes. Then count the Successes (a Critical counts as two) and the Trouble. Costs count for neither.

  • Successes ahead by 1: Lead +1. Ahead by 2 or more: Lead +2.
  • Trouble ahead by 1: Lead −1. Ahead by 2 or more: Lead −2.
  • Level: the Lead stays.

For example, a Success, a Critical, a Cost and a Trouble make 3 Successes to 1 Trouble: Lead +2.

Escape, and everyone in the flight is home with what they carry; anyone still in the lock-up is left behind. If you’re cornered, the party is forked, and the year’s result is Forked.

Once the Hunt Is On

From the moment the hunt is on, when the final flight begins:

  • Suspicion stops. Nothing raises it any more.
  • The Mask is off. Everyone rolls the Monster die.
  • Overdraw costs your Weakness instead of Suspicion. From your own next roll to the end of the flight, your trait die is one size smaller (if you overdraw on your own roll, that roll doesn’t take it). If you overdraw on the roll of someone who rolls before you in a round, your own roll that round already takes it.
  • You can overdraw only once per flight, and not at all while your Weakness is in play, however it came. (An Always Weakness is in play from round 1, so Dracula and a Ghost can’t overdraw in the flight.)

None of this applies in a local chase, where an overdraw costs Suspicion +2 as usual, on top of the chase’s one rise.
