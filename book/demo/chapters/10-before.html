For the Storyteller

Before You Play

This is a complete evening of Don’t Get Forked for 3–5 players who have never played. It teaches the game as it goes: nobody needs to read the rulebook first, you included. Read this page and the next before your guests arrive; everything after that you can read aloud at the table.

How to Read This Script

  • Boxes marked “Read aloud” are for the players. Read them word for word.
  • Grey, smaller text is for you alone: what to ask, what to roll, what to write down.
  • The Places has a page for every place in town. When the party goes somewhere, turn to it.
  • When It Happens has a box for everything the dice can do: a Cost, Trouble, a chase, a capture, a Tell, the final flight. When it happens, turn to it and read.
  • The rules here are the rulebook’s, said in plain words. If you have the book, it agrees; if a question comes up that this script doesn’t answer, make a quick ruling and keep the night moving.

What You Need

  • Each player: one each of d4, d6, d8, d10 and d12, plus a second d6 (the Mask) and a second d10 (the Monster). Players can share if you’re short.
  • You: a d6 (for Tells and chases) and this script.
  • The Entity cards (The Eight Entities, near the end): one per Entity, with the rulebook’s defaults already picked. Print them and cut them out, so players can choose. The Castle Duty can be changed at the table (Part One).
  • The tracker (the last page): print it. It keeps Suspicion, the Turns, the Lead, the shopping list and the lock-up. A coin or a token on each track is enough.
  • The map of Hallowby (Hallowby at a Glance), on the table where everyone can see it. Players move a token or a coin to show where their Entity is.

The Evening at a Glance

20–25 minPart One: The Castle. Who the players are, the shopping list, choosing Entities and Castle Duties, a practice roll.
45–90 minPart Two: Into Hallowby. The raid: twelve Turns of sneaking, rolling, and maybe running.
0–15 minThe final flight, if the town turns out with pitchforks.
5–10 minPart Three: How the Year Went. What came home, and the epilogue.

Take a short break after Part One if your group likes one: it’s the natural pause before the raid.

The Night in Numbers

Hallowby is an Easy town. Keep these to hand (they’re on the tracker too):

  • The shopping list: 4 items. One is essential: the cask of red wine.
  • Turns: 12, then dawn. Charges: 3 for each Entity.
  • Suspicion: starts at 0. The Limit is 11: when Suspicion reaches it, the whole town hunts the party (the final flight).
  • The way out: Difficulty 6. The lock-up: Difficulty 10.
  • A local chase: the Lead starts at 1, escape at 4, against a mob of 8 plus half the Suspicion (rounded down, at most 12).
  • The final flight: the Lead starts at 2, escape at 5, against a mob of 10.

Running a Turn

The raid is twelve Turns. Each Turn, run this loop. It is on the tracker too.

  1. Say the Turn: “Turn 3.” (The marker moves at the end of each Turn: step 5.)
  2. Go round the table. Ask each player, “What does your Entity do?” Each takes one action: roll at the next obstacle where it is, move to another place, pick up dropped items, or wait. They act one at a time, in any order they like, and each result counts at once.
  3. A move takes the Turn: the Entity is there now. Turn to that place’s page and read its arrival box. If it’s a watched place that nobody has reached yet (the way out: the first time anyone comes back to it), make a Tell check once everyone moving there this Turn has arrived; if you’re not sure who else is coming, ask before the next roll (When It Happens).
  4. A roll: ask which trait, Mask or Monster, and whether anyone spends a charge. Then they roll, you read the result, and you mark any Suspicion. One roll raises Suspicion once, by its biggest trigger.
  5. End of the Turn: if the mirror has been taken and is still in town, Suspicion +1. Then move the Turn marker. If that was Turn 12, dawn comes (When It Happens).

Whenever Suspicion reaches 11, stop wherever you are and turn to The Final Flight.

Keeping it moving

  • Let the players plan out loud, but if a Turn stalls, ask the quietest player first.
  • The party may split up. Hallowby is small enough to visit together, but splitting saves Turns.
  • When a player asks “can I…?”, the answer is usually “yes, if you get past the obstacle”. Every place is in this script; nothing else needs a roll.
  • Anyone with nothing to do, waiting at the bridge or in the lock-up, can voice a villager: Granny Mott, the constable, a judge from the costume contest.
  • Be generous with the funny voices and stingy with the rules talk: the script holds the rules, so you don’t have to.
