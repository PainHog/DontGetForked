Chapter 3

Rolling the Dice

You roll only to get past an obstacle (the way out and the lock-up are obstacles too) and in each round of a chase. Every roll is your trait die plus your Mask or Monster die, added together.

The Roll

  1. The obstacle sets the trait. Each obstacle lists the traits that work, usually one or two. When it lists two, one is usually marked as the loud way, and rolling that one raises Suspicion by 1, whatever the result. To roll any other trait you need an ability (below). In a chase, the ground sets the trait instead.
  2. Choose the second die, either the Mask d6 or the Monster d10. When you’re carrying furniture, or in the final flight, it must be the Monster.
  3. Roll both, add them, and compare the total with the obstacle’s Difficulty, which is 6, 8, 10 or 12.

Results

  • Success: the total is equal to the Difficulty or more. You do it.
  • Cost: 1–2 short. You do it, but the Storyteller picks one of these costs.
    • Suspicion +1.
    • Drop an item. The Storyteller picks one item of loot you carry, but never the loot this roll wins, and never furniture.
    • Lose a Turn, which means skipping your next action. If the hunt starts first, it lapses.
    • Your next roll’s trait die is one size smaller, whatever that roll is, a chase’s included.
    The Storyteller never picks a cost that costs nothing right then. For example, Suspicion +1 costs nothing on a roll that already raises Suspicion, because one roll raises it only once.
  • Trouble: 3 or more short. It doesn’t happen, and Suspicion rises by 1. If the obstacle is watched, you are also caught and must run for it in a local chase, as Chapter 6 describes.
  • Critical: a Success where both dice show the same number. Outside a chase, the roller gets back one charge it has spent this raid (never more than it started with; an overdraw isn’t a charge). In a chase it counts as two Successes.

In a chase, a result moves the Lead instead. A Cost costs nothing there, and Trouble starts no new chase. A local chase still raises Suspicion, once, when it ends (Chapter 6).

The Monster Shows

If you rolled the Monster die and its number is higher than your trait die’s, the Monster shows: Suspicion +2, whatever the result. A tie, or the Mask, shows nothing. The monster did the work, so of course it showed.

Abilities

Using an ability costs one charge. At zero charges you can still use one by overdrawing: it costs Suspicion +2 instead of a charge, on top of anything else the roll raises. In the final flight it costs your Weakness instead, so your trait die is one size smaller to the end of the flight. Chapter 6 says when you may do this.

Every ability but the Draught does one of these four things.

  • Make your trait die one size bigger, a raise (a d8 becomes a d10; a d12 can’t go higher).
  • Use the ability’s trait instead of the one the obstacle calls for (in a chase, instead of the ground’s).
  • Roll the Monster die without risking Suspicion. If the Monster shows, that adds no Suspicion, but everything else on the roll still counts, including Trouble, the loud way, a Cost picked as Suspicion and an overdraw.
  • Open an approach of your own. At an obstacle that doesn’t list the ability’s trait, roll that trait at a Difficulty 2 lower (a 10 becomes an 8). Never in a chase.
    • Any obstacle will do, not only a location’s first one (its way in).
    • The obstacle is still watched or not, as marked, so Trouble at a watched one still gets you caught.
    • Only you get past it. It isn’t beaten for the others, who must beat it themselves.
    • Two places work as usual. At the way out, your Success or Cost gets everyone out. At the lock-up, a rescuer’s Success or Cost frees every captive, and a captive may open one to slip free.

Spend abilities before you roll.

  • You can spend one on any Entity’s roll at the same place as you (a location, the way out or the lock-up), even if you haven’t got past the obstacles it has. In a local chase, only on your own roll.
  • Helping costs the charge but doesn’t take your action.
  • The roller always rolls its own dice, so Brute Force spent on a friend’s roll means the friend rolls its own Brawn.
  • You can open an approach, or drink the Draught, only on your own roll.
  • In the final flight, any ability may be spent as a raise instead of doing what it usually does.

Several abilities may go on one roll, but only one of them may change the trait, and the roll takes at most one raise from any source, Castle Duty included. A step down is anything that makes your trait die one size smaller, which means a Cost, your Weakness or carrying furniture (your Nimble only). Any number may apply. Net raises against steps down first. A d4 stepped down and raised stays a d4, and a d12 raised and stepped down stays a d12. Then apply the limits, so nothing makes a die smaller than a d4, or bigger than a d12.
